Battle screen layout

Two convoys running the same direction on a wide freeway, seen from behind. Each convoy drives in two lanes, inside and outside, three slots deep: ahead, center, behind. The two inside lanes meet in the middle, where the fight is closest. Past each convoy's outside lane is a shoulder, and it belongs to the other side: it's where their flankers pull alongside you. Both sides get the same space, so a friendly convoy reads as well as a raider pack.

Drawn in logical pixels at a 1280x720 reference and scaled by one number. Pick a viewport, a scenario, and a state. The fit check under the frame measures every text box, vehicle, chip, and card, and reports what overflows, collides, shrinks, or leaves the frame.

The road

Two lanes each, and the other side's shoulder

Lanes run across the screen and rows run up it, because everyone is driving up the screen. A convoy's own formation is six slots, inside and outside by ahead, center, and behind. A shoulder only ever holds vehicles from the far side that outran you and swerved around your outside lane, in the row of the vehicle they outran. Nobody parks on their own shoulder, and the fit check fails any scenario that breaks either rule. Slots never move or resize during a fight, so moving is a swerve from one slot to another and the road is a board you can plan on.

Numbers are the range from your inside-center slot: lanes apart plus rows apart. Your inside to their inside on the same row is 1, one row ahead or behind adds 1, and anything 3 or more away is out of reach for today's cards. A flanker on their shoulder is 1 from their outside lane and 2 from their inside, the rules' "flanking to front is range 2" kept intact. Straight grid distance, with no diagonal shortcut.

Worst cases

Checked, not assumed

With one vehicle per slot the road's worst case is simple: all eighteen slots full, six in each formation and three flanking on each shoulder. The other scenarios cover what the rules produce around that: the opening state, a passenger after a wreck, and hands that grow past five. Each runs at every viewport in five states (planning, inspecting the leftmost and rightmost cards, targeting, end-turn preview) and is measured for text overflow, collisions between vehicles, chips, and cards, tokens scaled below full size, and anything outside the frame.

Layout rules

One layout, one scale

Scale

s = min(W / 1280, H / 720), floored at 0.8. The logical canvas is never smaller than 1280x720.

  • 1920x1080: s 1.5. Rules text lands at 18px, the Xbox accessibility floor for PC.
  • 1280x800 (Steam Deck): the extra 80 goes to the rows, and tokens grow to fill them, up to x1.25.
  • 2560x1080: the stage caps at 1600 and centers; the road runs to the screen edges as art.
  • 1024x768: s 0.8, 1280x960 logical, so the rows are tall and tokens grow.
  • Below 0.8 (phone landscape) needs its own compact layout, not in this proposal.

Decided

Rules calls, made for how the game feels to play

A flanker takes the row it outran

A flank is aimed at someone. The flanker lands on the far shoulder in the row of the vehicle it outran, and the fit check fails any flanker with no opposing vehicle in its row.

The flanker's old slot stays empty

Nobody else in your convoy moves. Flanking should cost something, and the cost is the hole it leaves, which raiders can exploit. Sliding other vehicles to close up would move pieces you didn't choose to move, and a plan you made last turn would stop matching the board. When the flanker loses its speed edge it drops back into that reserved slot (Combat Rules already sends a flanker home when it's no longer faster).

Range is lanes apart plus rows apart

Straight grid distance, no diagonal shortcut. It keeps the rules' own numbers (inside to inside is 1, a flanker to their inside is 2) and gives ahead and behind a real job: pull ahead of a rammer to get out of its reach, drop behind to line up a shot. Counting diagonals as 1 would make rows nearly meaningless with only 1 and 2 range cards.

Every card has a short and a full text

The card face shows the short text, three lines built from keywords, so a hand stays scannable. The full text can say everything and lives in a detail view: hover with a mouse, focus with keyboard or controller, or long-press on touch. Right-click (or I, or the controller's inspect button) pins it open so you can read while looking at the road. Deck, discard, and reward screens use the same detail view. Both fields are required in cards.json, and the card data lint checks each against its budget.

Hand cap: 7 per driver

Draws past 7 go straight to discard with a visible burn, the way Slay the Spire handles 10. Nitro Boost still nets you cards up to the cap, so drawing stays exciting, but a hand never gets so wide you can't read it: 7 cards keep about 68px of each card visible, enough for most names. The big-hands scenario now tests 7.

Two driven vehicles, the rest are escorts

Two hands is the Symbiotic Driver System, and it's already a lot to hold in your head. Three hands means 15 cards a turn, the load Across the Obelisk players complain about with four heroes, and it would squeeze each card to about 39px. So the convoy grows through escorts: vehicles with plates and slots but no hand, that you order with cards from your drivers' decks. A third human player is co-op, not a third hand.

Two adrenaline pools, two decks

Each driver keeps their own deck, hand, and adrenaline, as Combat Rules and the code already do. Pooling them would erase the choice of which driver acts, which is the whole symbiosis. Game Flow's single "3/3" readout and shared draw pile are the outdated half of the docs.

A wreck makes a passenger, not a spectator

When a vehicle dies its driver rides with the partner, keeps their hand, and can't attack. Sitting out for the rest of the fight (Gameplay's "Lone Wolf" version) is dull in single player and awful in co-op. This also answers DDB-96: the hand keeps refilling.

Flanking is +50%, and the same driver twice is not allowed

+50% matches the cards and the code; 2x on top of the range advantage flanking already buys would make it the only play. Picking one driver for both slots (DDB-98) defeats a system built on two different drivers helping each other, so driver selection should refuse it.
