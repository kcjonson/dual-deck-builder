## Battle screen design (decided 2026-09-25, road model built)

The combat screen's layout and the rules it depends on are decided and written down. The combat model's half of the road is built (DDB-128 and DDB-129, one PR); the screen isn't. Spec: [docs/specs/Battle Screen Design.md](./specs/Battle%20Screen%20Design.md). Decision record, options, and rationale: [battle-screen-road-model.md](./AI_TECHNICAL_DECISIONS/battle-screen-road-model.md). Interactive mock with a fit matrix over six scenarios, six viewports, and five states: [docs/design/battle-screen/index.html](./design/battle-screen/index.html), served through any static server.

- The road: both convoys drive the same direction up the screen; two formation lanes by three rows (ahead, center, behind) per side, plus a shoulder per side that only holds the other team's flankers; one vehicle per slot, slots fixed. Range is lanes apart plus rows apart. A flanker takes the row it outran and its old slot stays empty.
- Rules calls made with it and written into Combat Rules: hand cap 7 per driver, two driven vehicles plus escorts, two adrenaline pools and two decks, a wrecked vehicle's driver becomes a passenger who can't attack, flanking +50%, no duplicate driver.
- Cards gain a required `summary` (card face, three lines) beside `description` (detail view, 330 characters). Eleven of the eighteen current descriptions don't fit three lines.
- DDB-131: `summary` is in `CardData`, `cards.json` (all 18 cards), and the loader; `src/renderer/game/data/cards.test.ts` checks descriptions against 330 characters and measures each summary at three lines on the card face, which shows it since DDB-71.
- Built: `mechanics/Road.ts` holds the lanes, rows, range formula, lane rules, and opening fill order. `Vehicle.slot` (null until a `Battle` places it) replaced `VehiclePosition`, `Vehicle.isFlanking` is derived from the slot, and `Vehicle.flank` carries a flanker's reserved slot and the vehicle it outran. `Battle` owns every move: opening placement in the constructor (encounter-given slots kept, the rest filled inside first, center, behind, ahead), `getFlankBlocker`/`canFlank`, the flank itself, and `dropBackFlankers` at the end of each player and enemy turn. Flank and Flanking Maneuver now target the vehicle they outrun.
- Enemy intents are planned in the model (DDB-132): each raider commits its whole turn at the start of the player's turn against a projection of slots, flank state, and speed, and the enemy turn plays that plan. Cards the player makes illegal fizzle, and cards aimed at a vehicle wrecked mid-turn follow its driver. Elites and bosses hide values. `Battle.getIntents(raider)` is the API for the intent pills and end-turn preview (DDB-33); the current screen shows each raider's first intent. Record: [enemy-intent-planning.md](./AI_TECHNICAL_DECISIONS/enemy-intent-planning.md).
- Escorts are designed, not built (DDB-133): undriven vehicles ordered with ORDER cards from the drivers' decks, once per turn, up to four in formation, plus ambush starts (raiders and set-piece escorts only) that let a side reach nine. Spec: Combat Rules' Escorts section and Card System Design 1.3 and 4.5. Record, with the proposals made while writing it up and the open questions, all answered by DDB-151 (build tasks DDB-146 to DDB-155): [escorts.md](./AI_TECHNICAL_DECISIONS/escorts.md).
- Ambush starts are built for raiders (DDB-147): a raider whose encounter gives it a slot on the player's shoulder starts flanking with `flank = { reservedSlot: null, outran: null }` and holds the shoulder at drop-back. `Battle.getAmbushBlocker` is the placement rule, for the opening and for reinforcement waves once they exist. DDB-146 opened `Battle.mayAmbush` to set-piece escorts on the player side.
- Wrecks and passengers (DDB-96): a wrecked vehicle's driver rides on as a passenger with their own hand, discard, and adrenaline, and the combat screen shows both drivers' hands from fixed seats, a passenger's attacks disabled. A wreck can't be targeted, holds its slot for the rest of the turn it died in, and leaves its team and the road at that turn's end (`Battle.clearWrecks`). Driver death (DDB-156): the dead leave their seat the moment their HP hits 0, a living passenger takes the wheel, and dead drivers don't draw, refill, or play. A raider left with nobody alive aboard is out of the fight: untargetable, no seat for wreck survivors, and gone from the road at the end of the turn. A player's vehicle becomes an escort instead (DDB-152).
- Escort model built (DDB-146): `Vehicle.escort` holds the profile, `ESCORT_CONFIGS` and `createEscort` in `mechanics/Escort.ts` the four starting types, and a player team takes 2 driven vehicles plus up to 4 convoy escorts (set-piece allies don't count), placed at their preferred slots after the drivers. Set-piece escorts may ambush. Escorts are never unmanned, so they stay targetable and on the road.
- Escorts take fire (DDB-148): `Battle.checkHit` is the one hit rule for play, planning, and the AI, reading each side's skills from `Vehicle.crewSkills` (an escort's own profile, otherwise the driver's). Past armor an empty escort takes everything on structure; with a passenger it splits like a driven vehicle. Wrecked escorts leave at the end of the turn like any wreck. An empty escort isn't a legal Headshot target; one with a passenger is, and Headshot hits the passenger (escorts.md decision 22, a proposal for Kevin).
- Order cards built (DDB-149): nine orders in `cards.json` (six from the spec plus the Outrider's Run Ahead, Pilot Car's Flag Down, and Fuel Hauler's Top Off, decided 2026-09-26 as escorts.md decisions 23 to 27). `BoardProjection.orderCarrier` picks the nearest ready escort in range with the spec's ties; `Vehicle.spent` resets each player turn; Draw Fire redirects single-target intents as they play and in the preview. Not built here: deck insertion (DDB-153) and the plate's passenger row for picking who aboard. Close Ranks and Draw Fire grant Shield (decision 28): temporary, uncapped, stacking armor that soaks damage first and clears at the start of each player turn. Whether Armor Plating and Repair Kit move to Shield is DDB-164.
- Raider target preferences (DDB-150): `Vehicle.raiderArchetype` is looter, killer, or unset, independent of the AI strategy. `AIPlayer.getValidTargets` narrows `targetBlocker`'s legal targets to the preferred ones (haulers for a looter, driven vehicles for a killer) when any is legal, for single-target cards that land something on their target, so marks, preview, and play agree. The Rust Buggy is a looter. Draw Fire never pulls an area intent (escorts.md decisions 29 and 30).
- Escort persistence and dividends (DDB-151): the roster lives in `Convoy` (`mechanics/Convoy.ts`), the only run state so far: escorts in roster order, `add` up to four, `dismiss` (takes the escort's signature copy out of the drivers' cards), and `afterFight`, which drops the lost and appends anything that became an escort mid-fight, even past four (`isOverCap`, for a future garage to resolve). `Battle.endCombat` returns an `AfterFight`: lost convoy escorts, whose `broughtBy` copies leave every pile; every living player vehicle off the road via `Vehicle.leaveRoad`, convoy escorts carrying only structure (armor refills, a proposal), seats left as they are; and dividends after a won fight only. Med Truck heals every living driver 3 (decision 32), applied by the battle; Fuel Hauler's +1 fuel is in the result, since nothing holds fuel or scrap yet. A converted driven vehicle stays in the convoy (decision 33). Salvage Rig isn't built (no signature card yet).
- Passengers and unmanned vehicles (DDB-152): a player wreck's survivors go to the partner's driven vehicle, then the escort nearest the wreck (attack-order ties), else crash out; raiders keep "first free seat". Every living vehicle not an unmanned raider has a seat, escorts included. `Team.handleDriverDeath` turns a player vehicle with nobody at the wheel into an escort (`convertToEscort`: `UNMANNED_CREW` gunnery 4, evade 3, ramming 2, base speed, no signature, starts spent); raiders stay out and leave at turn end. Plates rebuild when a vehicle's shape changes. Record: escorts.md decisions 40 to 47.
- Draw Fire covers the row (DDB-169, escorts.md decision 48): `Battle.drawFireRedirect` pulls an aimed intent at any other player vehicle in the fight in the covering escort's row, haulers, escorts, and converted vehicles included, so it counters looters as well as killers. A shot at the covering escort itself lands as planned. Everything else in decision 20 is unchanged.
- The intent preview judges each raider from where it will be when its card plays (DDB-170): `Battle.projectEnemyTurn` drops back flankers the player outpaced and replays the plans on a `BoardProjection`, so Draw Fire redirects, range, and the flank bonus in the preview match play. An intent that will fizzle drops out of the preview and changes nothing for later raiders, formula damage uses the target's projected speed, and planning starts from the same drop-back (`Battle.projectEnemyTurnStart`), so a flanker whose boost wore off doesn't plan from a shoulder it's expected to lose.
- EMP Blast stuns (DDB-173): a stunned raider skips its enemy turn. `Vehicle.isStunned` is the one check, read by planning (`AIController.planEnemyTurn`), the preview (`Battle.projectEnemyTurn`), and play (`processEnemyTurns`, which logs the skip). The duration 1 stun ticks off at the start of the player's turn, before raiders plan, so it covers exactly one enemy turn. A stunned raider still takes damage, can be targeted, and drops back when outpaced.
- Driver speed counts once (DDB-159): `Vehicle.speed` is base speed plus the speed skill (`skills.speed`) of whoever is at the wheel plus statuses, and every driven vehicle comes from `createDrivenVehicle`. Stock totals are Rig 2, Workshop 4, Spike Buggy 5, Bike 8, against escorts at 2 to 5. Per-driver values are in Combat Rules, Driver speeds.
- AI speed and kill scoring (DDB-163, DDB-168): there's no fixed "fast" speed. An AI is fast enough to flank when `BoardProjection.canFlankAnyone` says it can outrun someone still in the fight (the flank check, speed bonus optional), and a rammer's edge is its speed over the target. The projection keeps `Vehicle.speedSum` and floors at 0 only when read, as play does. Every AI kill or effective-HP estimate goes through `ai/DamageEstimate.ts`: `damageToFinish` (driver HP for driver-only damage, bare structure for structure-only, otherwise `Vehicle.damageToWreck`: Shield, armor, then structure with the crew's half), and `lastingDamage` drops what Shield soaks. Raiders plan right after Shield clears, so in play today they never see Shield on a target.
- AI strategies check only effect types the cards use (DDB-171): `change_position` is a flank and a speed status on whoever acts is a boost, judged through `canFlankAnyone` with the summed `selfSpeedBonus`; MCTS scores the two once per card for the actor, the Outrider `orderCarrier` picks for Run Ahead, and credits a flanking card's boost only through the flank. `draw_cards`, `gain_armor`, and `gain_resource` (adrenaline) replace the old `draw`, `armor`, and `adrenaline` names. MCTS values a slow on its target at 0.35 per point of speed it can take (floored at 0) and armor only up to max; Ramming doesn't value slows. `ai/__tests__/StrategyEffectTypes.test.ts` fails if any AI file checks an effect type, applied status, or resource missing from `cards.json`. `Battle.ts` still accepts the old alias names; BoardProjection and Vehicle's dead names are DDB-176.
- Open on the road model: the old battlefield layers draw each team in three columns by lane kind until DDB-134.
- Hand cap 7 is enforced in `Driver.drawCards` (DDB-130): draws past it go to discard, emit `cardsBurned` for the UI, and log in the battle log.
- `DriverLoader` never exposes its templates (DDB-157): every getter returns fresh copies, so combat can mutate its drivers freely and each run starts clean.
- No duplicate driver is enforced (DDB-98): the driver-selection panels skip each other's driver, and `CombatScreen.initializeCombat` rejects a same-archetype pair through `mechanics/DriverPair.ts`. `Team` doesn't check it yet because the AI and battle test fixtures field two Road Warriors.
- Combat screen layout (DDB-124): `computeCombatLayout` in `screens/combat/CombatLayout.ts` is the one layout for mount and every resize, and `CombatScreen` builds its UI in `onMount`. Each combat layer lays out its own children the same way on construction and resize, so a resized screen matches one mounted at that size. The bands still stop at 88% of the height; what fills the bottom 12% is open for Kevin and the redesign (DDB-127).
- Bugs from the 2026-09-25 capture and playthrough are DDB-111 to DDB-126 under DDB-5. Build work is its own epic on Specboard; the UI half is sequenced behind DDB-55's phases 4 to 6 (DDB-82 now builds these bands, DDB-88 the dock, detail view, and targeting).

## Phase 1: the draw core (complete, 2026-09-28)

The seam lands before the machine, per `.claude/notes/ddb55-phase1-recon.md`. DDB-65 is split into four PRs: the draw API with no consumers, then `LegacyGLBackend` plus every consumer moved onto it (DDB-67), then the batcher, then the clip-stack remainder, all four merged. The WebGL2 backend (DDB-63) followed them, then the resource layer and viewport owner (DDB-66, with DDB-186's feature detection); then the uber shader (DDB-64), the phase's first re-baseline, and the ordering re-baseline (DDB-67), the second.

**PR 1 has landed: `src/renderer/engine/draw/`, eighteen files, no consumers, nothing else in the repo touched.** Chapter 2's whole surface, the four state stacks, R2.21's null backend and R2.22's recording backend. The design decision everything downstream inherits is that a draw call reads the four stacks once and stamps the resolved values onto an inert plain-data `DrawCommand`, so a backend never sees a stack, a push, or a pop; the alternatives considered and eleven named departures are in [draw-api-state-resolution.md](./AI_TECHNICAL_DECISIONS/draw-api-state-resolution.md).

Two chapter 3 and 4 rules landed here rather than with the batcher, both because leaving them out would have made this PR's tests lie. R3.10's per-layer partition runs upstream of the backend, since R2.22 says the recording backend returns "the sorted draw list" and 3.12's required order tests are false-green without a sort. R4.2a's CPU cull is implemented, since `culled` is a live R13.12 counter and chapter 4 rates the cull required. Neither is the batcher: nothing merges geometry, drops a group as occluded, or splits a GPU submission.

**PR 3 has landed: `LegacyGLBackend`, and every consumer moved onto the draw API in the same commit.** The old GL drawing path moved bodily out of `Renderer` and behind `DrawBackend`; `Renderer` is now a device (today: canvas, WebGL2 context, resize, context loss, `getContext`, `getFontAtlas`; the matrices, `clear` and `useShader` it had here moved into `WebGL2Backend` with DDB-63) with no drawing methods, roughly 500 of 697 lines gone. `Rectangle`, `Circle`, `Polygon`, `Triangle`, `Text` and `Layer`'s background draw through `RendererContext.getInstance().draw`; `Layer` and `Panel` clip through `pushClip`/`popClip`; both frame loops (`src/index.ts` via `Game`, and `src/gallery/index.ts`, which drives `SceneHost` and never touches `Game`) open and close the frame through the same `createLegacyDrawApi` factory and the same `windowFrame()` helper. `getParameter(SCISSOR_BOX)` and its save-and-restore are gone from both clip sites, which is R15.22 conformance. `Screen`, `ScreenManager` and the seven screen subclasses lost a `Renderer` parameter no screen ever read. Zero visual change: 34 passed / 4 skipped across both Playwright projects, unchanged, with no baseline written.

**`DrawApiOptions.legacyTextOrder` was the one temporary thing in that PR.** It ended a sort domain at every clip push and pop to reproduce the flush points `Renderer.enableScissor` and `disableScissor` had, which the goldens of the time were pictures of and which R2.2 names as this engine's bug. DDB-67 deleted it, with `LegacyPaintOrder`, and re-minted; see the paint order paragraph below and [legacy-gl-backend.md](./AI_TECHNICAL_DECISIONS/legacy-gl-backend.md) for the experiments behind it.

Three measurements from that PR worth carrying forward. The barrier is worth exactly three goldens: with the flag off, `developerScreen`, `cardShowcaseScreen` and `combatScreen` fail and the other eighteen chromium specs pass, because those three are the only screens that clip. No clip nests inside another in any of the 14 captured states, instrumented and driven rather than reasoned about, which is what makes R4.3's intersection indistinguishable from the old replace-then-restore today and is the claim to re-check when a screen changes. And the four "GPU stall due to ReadPixels" warnings on the card showcase are **not** the scissor read-back: that branch never executes there, because nothing nests. Bisecting found `TextRenderer.flush`; suppressing the text flush took the count from 4 to 0 at the time. `TextRenderer` has since been deleted by the batcher PR, and whether the warnings went with it has not been re-measured: the macOS Chromium used for that PR's checks emits no such warning even for a deliberate `readPixels`, so it cannot observe them either way. Earlier notes attributing those warnings to `Layer.ts:453` and `Panel.ts:323` were wrong.

**The batcher (third PR): one GPU draw per sort domain, byte-identical pixels.** `Batcher` (behind the seam, composed by a backend with its own `GeometryEncoder`) merges each domain's groups into one upload and splits a GPU draw only for a named reason; `ResidentTextureSet` is R5.20's resident units. The legacy WebGL1 shader's per-draw uniforms became per-vertex attributes computed exactly as before, text joined shapes in the same buffer, and `TextRenderer` is deleted. GPU draws: combat 91 to 6, card showcase 61 to 2, developer 19 to 2, main menu 7 to 1. All six reachable screens and seven baselined gallery scenes hash byte-identically against `main`. `window.__perf.snapshot().batcher` is now `DrawApi.getStats()` rather than null, and `batcher.gpuDraws` matches `renderer.glDrawCalls`. Still open: the clip is scissor state (per-draw clip data waits on DDB-64, the encoder's `clipIsState` flag is the seam), `occluded` is null, `topologyChange` is a legacy-only split reason, and `legacyTextOrder` plus the new `legacyPaintOrder` both die in the ordering re-baseline. Details in [batcher.md](./AI_TECHNICAL_DECISIONS/batcher.md).

**The clip remainder (fourth PR): the clip stack's consumers agree with it.** `Layer.clipsChildren` is the one fact render, snapshot and hit test read. `Panel`'s subtree cull is gone in favour of R4.2a's, and text is now culled per run through an optional `DrawBackend.textInk` from the same glyph walk the encoder takes. `treeSnapshot` intersects with the clip stack's `intersectClip` and reports R4.2's `empty` as a zero-sized rect. R4.12's scrolled-out half ships: `containsPoint` rejects a point outside any clipping ancestor. Promotion's hit-test clip reset and reverse-paint-order dispatch wait on phase 3 (DDB-75). Pixels byte-identical against `main`. Details in [clip-stack-consumers.md](./AI_TECHNICAL_DECISIONS/clip-stack-consumers.md).

**The WebGL2 backend (DDB-63): the live path is WebGL2, pixels byte-identical.** `WebGL2Backend` replaced `LegacyGLBackend` and reuses the batcher and the legacy encoder (now on 32-bit indices); the two shaders are ported to GLSL ES 3.00 line for line. A fixed-capacity vertex ring that wraps only onto regions two frames old (`StreamRing`, R5.27) and, since DDB-195, an index buffer pool with one buffer per upload, a `std140` frame block in a ring of three slots bound with `bindBufferRange`, one vertex array, the atlas on `texStorage2D`, no synchronous GL call in the frame (a recording-context unit test enforces R15.22), and context loss: `FrameLoop` stops, a DOM status line says why, and the backend and the atlas rebuild on restore (`tests/visual/web/contextLoss.spec.ts` checks the restored frame against the combat golden). The clear moved into the backend's `beginFrame`. Two measured departures from R15.2 and R15.25 stay until DDB-64 re-mints: `antialias: true` (off moves 12 of 13 goldens) and SRC_ALPHA blending. `primitive-shapes` has its golden now that `Polygon` ear-clips the concave star (DDB-185). R15.3 is DDB-186 and R15.4 is DDB-66. Details in [webgl2-backend.md](./AI_TECHNICAL_DECISIONS/webgl2-backend.md).

**The resource layer and the viewport owner (DDB-66, DDB-186): no pixel moves at ratio 1.** `gpu/TextureStore.ts` owns every texture: reference counts, uploads queued and drained at `beginFrame` under a 2 MB budget, storage allocated at upload, and context-loss recovery from a kept source, a `reload()`, or nothing for an empty texture; a texture with contents and neither is reported lost. `WebGL2TextureDevice` is the one `texStorage2D` caller and uploads on unit 31 so it never disturbs a draw binding. The font atlas is its first texture; the null and recording backends count through the same class. `DrawBackend.textures` replaced `createTexture`/`destroyTexture` on the seam, and `DrawApi` drives the store's frame and fills `residentTextureBytes`, `pendingUploads` and `evictions` (a measured zero). `CanvasViewport` is R7.11's one owner: a `ResizeObserver` on `device-pixel-content-box` with the WebKit fallback `Math.round(cssSize * dpr)`, a re-registered `matchMedia` resolution query, logical size defined as framebuffer over `dpr * uiScale`, and changes committed at the top of the next frame, where the GL viewport, then `ScreenManager.resize`, then the gallery host hear them. `windowFrame()` and every window `resize` listener are gone; the canvas's CSS box is the page's (`width/height: 100%`). `coords/snapping.ts` has R7.7 to R7.8a's arithmetic; the legacy encoder's glyph rounding goes through `snapToDevice`, which is `Math.round` at ratio 1. Hairline and clip snapping are not applied yet (DDB-64's encoder is where they belong). The perf snapshot carries `device` (backend, unmasked GPU identity, timer query and parallel compile support). Details in [resource-layer-and-viewport.md](./AI_TECHNICAL_DECISIONS/resource-layer-and-viewport.md).

**The uber shader (DDB-64): one program, coverage in the fragment stage, the clip as data. First re-baseline.** `uber.vert` and `uber.frag` replace the legacy pair: modes `flat`, `rect`, `shadow`, `circle`, `image`, `text` and a temporary `mask` for the bitmap atlas, rounded-box SDFs with R5.8's exact-coverage border compositing, the linear one-pixel ramp on quads inflated a device pixel, erf shadows with three sigma of padding, premultiplied output blended `ONE, ONE_MINUS_SRC_ALPHA`, and the clip tested per fragment on a `highp` logical position, so there is no scissor. `UberGeometryEncoder` (four 128-byte vertices per quad) does the CPU half: transform, premultiplication, radii clamps, border outsets, shadow geometry, line quads, the polygon feather ring. The context is `antialias: false`. `Rectangle` passes the corner radius it always parsed, so buttons, inputs, panels and cards are rounded, and borders are their configured width on all four sides (the old UV-space border was 0.4 px on the long sides of a button). Every golden moved for those reasons and was re-minted. GPU draw counts are unchanged (combat 6, showcase 2), because the `legacyTextOrder` barrier is now the only thing that splits a domain: with it off every screen is one GPU draw. `clipChange` and `topologyChange` are gone as live counters. Real-shader pixel tests for chapter 4.7's fragment fixture and chapter 5.10 run in `tests/visual/web/uberShader.spec.ts`. The re-mint also caught a stale combat golden (the intent badge reads 15, `main`'s golden said 5; a two-glyph change is under the gate's 200-pixel budget, DDB-197). Not here: R7.8 and R5.10 snapping (DDB-188), R4.14's rounded clip (DDB-190), the packed instance layout (DDB-191), the developer screen's section titles sitting on their panel borders (DDB-196). Details in [uber-shader.md](./AI_TECHNICAL_DECISIONS/uber-shader.md).

**GPU timer, DevTools tracks and capture tables (DDB-92, phase 7): no pixel moves.** `rendering/GpuTimer.ts` issues one `TIME_ELAPSED_EXT` query per GPU pass (the clear and each sort domain), reads results no sooner than two frames later without ever waiting, drops samples across a disjoint event, over three times their CPU frame, or across a lost context, and falls back to a per-frame fence reported as `gpu.latencyMs` where the extension is absent. `WebGL2Backend` makes four calls on it and reads nothing; both pages build it in development builds only, disabled, and F5 or `window.__perf.gpuTimer(on)` turns it on. The snapshot's `gpu` block is live and gains `source`, `p99Ms`, `maxMs`, `sampleCount` and `invalidCount`; a top-level `tracks` says whether the frame sections reach DevTools as a custom track (`console.timeStamp` from Chromium 134, `performance.measure` from 128, null on Electron 25). The F5 overlay reads the same snapshot function and adds GPU, worst-section, device and track lines. `perf-capture.mjs` writes a markdown table beside each capture, compares two captures (R13.39), and has `--vsync on` for GPU captures because Chromium returns query results hundreds of frames late with the frame cap off; the timer runs only on paced captures. Baselines: `perf-results/phase7-{frame,gallery}-baseline` (vsync off, timer off) and `phase7-{frame,gallery}-gpu` (paced, timer on), all captured before the uber shader (#76) landed, so they are its before picture. On ANGLE Metal every timed pass reads at least 1.4 ms (one clear or twenty), so `gpu.ms` there overstates the work and compares only on the same device and pass count. Combat ran at 26 to 28 ms a frame (36 to 38 FPS) on the Radeon Pro 560X in these captures, paced or not, timer on or off: a regression from #70 (DDB-63), not the timer, bisected to the index ring's size and fixed by DDB-195 (next paragraph). Details in [gpu-timer-and-perf-capture.md](./AI_TECHNICAL_DECISIONS/gpu-timer-and-perf-capture.md).

**Index storage is a pool of per-upload buffers (DDB-195): combat back to 60 FPS, no pixel moves.** The WebGL2 backend's one index ring (DDB-63) cost combat 11 ms a frame on ANGLE Metal: a `drawElements` from a freshly written element buffer pays work proportional to the whole buffer, so every upload paid for three frames' worth of indices. The uber shader's stride change had shrunk the ring under the cliff, but a 4 MB ring still took combat to 33 ms. `rendering/IndexBufferPool.ts` gives each upload its own slot, the smallest free one that fits, reused only once two frames old (R5.27's ring-of-buffers shape), created at a power of two from 16 KB and never resized, with 24 small slots from the start so ordinary screens never allocate in a frame. The vertex stream stays one `StreamRing`. Combat measures 16.6 to 16.8 ms paced against 16.6 before DDB-63. Details in [index-buffer-pool.md](./AI_TECHNICAL_DECISIONS/index-buffer-pool.md).

**Perf follow-ups (DDB-192, DDB-193, DDB-194): no pixel moves, the overlay is still off by default.** The snapshot has `hitches`: long frames from `long-animation-frame` (or `longtask` on Electron 25) with the worst frame's longest script, and input slower than 16 ms from `event` entries with its delay, handler and presentation split, counted over the frame window's span and null where the runtime lacks the entry type (R15.29's second half, `debug/hitchObserver.ts`). `input` stays null because event entries only exist past the threshold. The F5 overlay shows both, anchors to `CanvasViewport`'s logical width on every committed change rather than `window.innerWidth` at startup, draws as its own domain after a barrier while shown (R3.21) so no screen draw paints over it, and the capture table has a "Long frames" column. The ANGLE Metal per-pass timer floor was the 4x MSAA resolve that each query boundary forced (`scripts/timer-floor-probe.mjs` reproduces it, single-query controls included): with `antialias: false` passes after the first read their own work, and one 1.26 ms full-target clear and store per frame remains in the clear pass (real work, 0.04 ms at 128x128), so per-pass queries stay. The four phase 7 baselines are recaptured after DDB-64 and DDB-195, each with a comparison table against the DDB-92 captures. Details in [gpu-timer-and-perf-capture.md](./AI_TECHNICAL_DECISIONS/gpu-timer-and-perf-capture.md).
