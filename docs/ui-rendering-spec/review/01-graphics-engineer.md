# Review R1: real-time graphics engineer

Draft under review: docs/ui-rendering-spec, second draft, 2026-09-07. Chapters read in full: README, 00, 01, 02, 03, 04, 05, 06, 07, 13, 15, 16; 08 to 12 skimmed for cross-references; research reports 01 and 05 read for context.

Status: complete. 37 findings (1 blocker, 16 major, 17 minor, 3 nit), a keep list, and a verification log.

Severity scale: blocker (the rule as written produces wrong pixels or cannot be implemented), major (a real correctness or performance hole that will cost a rewrite if found late), minor (fix before draft 3), nit (wording).

## Findings

Findings are grouped by chapter. Each carries id, severity, location, claim, problem, proposed change. "Ratio" below means device pixels per logical pixel (see G25: with `uiScale` it is `dpr * uiScale`, not `dpr`).

### Chapter 3, render order

#### G1. Effective layer can go down, and the ladder cannot express "above my host" (major)

- location: chapter 3, R3.5, R3.6, R3.9, R3.15
- claim: "A component that sets a layer promotes its whole subtree to that layer; descendants inherit it unless they set their own." Nine named layers in a fixed order.
- problem: Two holes. (a) Nothing forbids a descendant from setting a layer *lower* than its ancestor's effective layer. A `base` child of a `raised` card paints beneath the card's own background, and beneath every other `raised` and `base` group in the domain, which is never what an author means and is not diagnosable from the tree. CSS has no equivalent because a stacking context is atomic. (b) The ladder is absolute, so a host that already sits high has nowhere to send its own popup: a select inside a toast opens its menu in `popup` (below `toast`), a context menu on a drag ghost opens in `popup` (below `drag`), a tooltip on a ghost sits in `tooltip` (below `drag`). Each of those paints under its host. The worked example in 3.9 covers "toast over an open menu" but not "menu opened from a toast". Related: within one layer the order is tree order, so two open non-modal surfaces in different subtrees (two popovers, R12.33) cannot be brought to front by the user without reparenting; only overlay roots have open order.
- proposed change: Replace the inheritance sentence of R3.6 with: "A component's effective layer is `max(ownLayer, parent.effectiveLayer)` by ordinal, `inherit` counting as the parent's value. A subtree therefore never paints beneath its own ancestor's layer; a development build reports a component whose own layer is lower than its parent's effective layer as an authoring error." Add R3.6a: "A promoted subtree hosted inside a subtree that is already at or above the requested layer stays in the host's layer and relies on submission order (it is a later draw than its host). The overlay service (chapter 8, R8.21) MUST offer `bringToFront(root)` and MUST close exclusive popups (chapter 9, R9.14) when a modal root opens, so that a `popup` opened before a modal never paints above the scrim." Add to R3.9 one line per problematic pair: "menu from a toast: paints in `toast` above its host toast and beneath later toasts; the popup service SHOULD open such menus as overlay roots instead". Also cross-reference chapter 9: a promoted child keeps its host's content offset (R3.8), so a menu whose trigger scrolls out of the viewport floats detached; the popup service SHOULD close a popup when any ancestor scrolls.

#### G2. Group opacity is under-specified where it meets layers, clips, and domains (major)

- location: chapter 3, R3.26; chapter 4, R4.1; chapter 7, R7.13; chapter 8, R8.20
- claim: "An implementation MAY offer true group opacity by rendering a subtree to an off-screen target and compositing it once; if it does, that composite is a single draw in the owner's layer and position."
- problem: Four things are undefined and each produces a wrong image if guessed. (1) A promoted descendant (a `popup` child of a fading panel) is either rendered into the target and composited in the owner's layer (losing its promotion, and its clip reset means nothing inside a target) or left out (then it needs the scalar opacity instead). (2) R4.1 captures clips in *screen* space at submission; draws rendered into a target with a different origin or size carry clips in the wrong space, and R4.4's framebuffer-height flip uses the wrong height. (3) The subtree's draws cannot take part in the parent domain's sort (they have to be on the GPU before the composite quad is drawn), so a composite is a nested domain with its own flush, which R3.20 says nothing else may do. (4) Target format, clear colour, and premultiplication are unstated; compositing a non-premultiplied target, or one cleared to opaque black, gives dark fringes at every edge inside the group.
- proposed change: Replace R3.26's second sentence with: "An implementation MAY offer group opacity. If it does: (a) the subtree is rendered as a nested domain into an off-screen RGBA8 target that is cleared to transparent black and drawn premultiplied; the target is the subtree's `screenBounds` united with its `inkBounds`, intersected with the current clip, rounded outward to device pixels; its projection, clip space, and ratio are the target's (chapter 7, R7.13), and clips pushed inside are captured in the target's space. (b) Layers inside the nested domain sort among themselves only; a promoted descendant does not escape the group (CSS `opacity < 1` stacking-context semantics). An implementation that cannot honour that MUST fall back to scalar opacity for a subtree containing a promoted descendant rather than composite it. (c) The composite is one `image` draw in the owner's effective layer at the owner's submission position, tinted by the group opacity (tint alpha multiplies premultiplied colour and alpha together), carrying the owner's clip. (d) Targets are pooled and sized by power-of-two buckets, recreated on context loss (chapter 15, R15.5), and counted as `targetSwitches` (chapter 13, R13.13)." Add to 3.12 a required test: "a `popup` child inside an opacity group composites inside the group and does not appear after the base groups".

#### G3. Sorting by layer should be a bucket partition, not a stable sort (major, performance)

- location: chapter 3, R3.10, 3.12 tests; chapter 1, R1.5; chapter 13, R13.13
- claim: "at the barrier the groups are stable-sorted by layer and submitted", with "a single-layer fast path" that tests must "assert the sort did not run".
- problem: The key has at most nine values that are known at submission time (the batcher stamps the layer). A comparison sort is O(n log n) with a comparator call per compare, allocation-heavy in JavaScript (`Array.prototype.sort` on objects), and runs every frame a popup is open, which in a game UI is most frames with a menu, tooltip, or hovered `raised` card. Worldsim measured 94 us versus 282 us in C++ for 10k groups; a JavaScript comparator sort on 10k objects is closer to 1 to 2 ms. The conformance checklist and the required test both mandate the sort mechanism rather than the observable order, so the cheaper implementation is non-conforming as written.
- proposed change: Reword R3.10: "Within one layer of one domain the final order is submission order. The batcher MUST produce, per domain, the concatenation of the per-layer submission sequences in layer-ordinal order. The natural implementation appends each group's index (or instance) range to a pre-sized per-layer list at submission and concatenates the non-empty lists at flush: O(groups), stable by construction, no allocation in steady state. A comparison sort is acceptable only if it is stable." Replace the fast-path test with: "a domain whose groups all carry one layer uploads its submission sequence unchanged (no concatenation copy)". Keep `sortedGroups` in R13.13 but rename it `reorderedGroups` with the definition "groups emitted at a position different from their submission position".

#### G4. R3.2 forbids a free occlusion optimisation (minor)

- location: chapter 3, R3.2
- claim: "The 2D path MUST NOT partition draws into opaque and transparent passes. The cost is overdraw of panel backgrounds, which measured as negligible".
- problem: Negligible on worldsim's desktop; a 2880 by 1764 framebuffer is 5 M fragments per full-screen layer, and a modal over a screen over a world background is three to five of them, each about 25 ALU plus a blend, which is where integrated GPUs and 4k displays start to drop frames. The MUST NOT also forbids the harmless case: a fully opaque, unclipped, axis-aligned rect covering the whole target within a domain makes every earlier group in that domain invisible.
- proposed change: Keep the single ordering rule and add: "An implementation MAY drop groups submitted earlier in the same domain that lie entirely within a later group that is an opaque (alpha 1, no gradient with alpha, radius 0, no border alpha), unclipped, axis-aligned `rect` or `image`, since the painter's algorithm makes them invisible; this changes no pixel. Two-pass opaque/alpha rendering with a depth buffer remains prohibited." Count the dropped groups as `occluded` in R13.13.

#### G5. Promotion under a rotated ancestor inherits the rotation (minor)

- location: chapter 3, R3.8; chapter 8, R8.26; chapter 4, R4.7
- claim: Promotion resets the clip "and nothing else: not the coordinate origin, the content offset, the transform, opacity, or `visible`."
- problem: A popup that is a child of a fanned card (R8.26's own example) is drawn rotated by the card's transform, its text loses run snapping (R6.16 snaps only under translate-only transforms), and its placement rect from the placement service is converted back through `screenToLocal` into a rotated space where the axis-aligned answer no longer fits. Service-owned tooltips and drag ghosts are overlay roots and are unaffected; owner-child popups (select menus, popovers with `anchor` a component) are affected.
- proposed change: Add to R3.8: "A promoted component MAY set `detachTransform: true`, in which case its subtree is rendered and hit-tested in screen space with the identity transform, positioned by its `screenBounds`-derived placement result directly; the hit walk (chapter 8, R8.12) skips the inverse transform of ancestors for such a subtree. Default false. Popups owned by a transformed component SHOULD set it."

### Chapter 4, clipping

#### G6. The gl_FragCoord clip test drags ratio, target height, and a Y flip into the shader; test an interpolated logical position instead (major)

- location: chapter 4, R4.4; chapter 5, R5.28; chapter 7, R7.2; 4.7 test "DPR"
- claim: "The fragment test runs in device pixels. The implementation MUST either scale the bounds by the device pixel ratio before upload or pass the ratio and the framebuffer height to the shader".
- problem: Both options put the ratio and the target height into the shader, which is where two of worldsim's three recorded DPI bugs lived, and the second breaks silently for off-screen targets (G2) and for `uiScale` (G25) unless every flush re-uploads the right height and ratio. None of it is necessary. The vertex shader already has the draw's logical-space position (positions are submitted in screen-space logical pixels, R5.3); pass it down as a `highp vec2` varying and compare it with the logical clip rect. Under an orthographic projection the interpolated value at a fragment centre is that centre's logical coordinate to float precision, no flip, no ratio, no height, identical on any target. The rounded clip of R4.14 evaluates its SDF on the same varying. The half-open convention and the hard edge should also be stated: a clip edge at a fractional device coordinate is snapped by the test, so an animating clip edge (a resizing panel) shimmers by one pixel unless the clip rect is snapped on the CPU (G24).
- proposed change: Replace R4.4 with: "The fragment stage tests an interpolated logical-space position (`v_pos`, `highp`, the untransformed screen-space vertex position) against the logical clip rect with half-open edges: a fragment survives when `v_pos.x >= minX && v_pos.x < maxX && v_pos.y >= minY && v_pos.y < maxY`. No ratio, framebuffer height, or Y flip is involved; the test is identical for the window and for off-screen targets. The rectangular clip edge is not anti-aliased; a clip rect that is not on the device pixel grid discards or keeps whole pixels by their centres, so containers SHOULD snap their clip rects (chapter 7, R7.8a)." Change the 4.7 DPR test to: "with ratio 2, a clip at logical (10, 10, 20, 20) keeps the device pixel whose centre is (20.5, 20.5) and discards the one at (19.5, 19.5) and the one at (40.5, 40.5)". Drop "framebuffer height (for the clip flip)" and "device pixel ratio" from R5.28's per-flush uniform list; the ratio is still needed by the rounded clip's one-pixel edge ramp and can be taken from `fwidth(v_pos)` for free.

#### G7. No rule culls draws that lie entirely outside the clip (major, performance)

- location: chapter 4, R4.1, R4.2; chapter 6, R6.18
- claim: Clipped geometry is uploaded and the fragment stage discards; the only CPU drop is the `empty` clip state.
- problem: A scrolled list of 500 rows submits 500 backgrounds and 500 text runs of which 12 are visible; every glyph quad of the other 488 rows is transformed, snapped, written to the vertex array, uploaded, rasterised, and discarded per fragment. The stress scene of R13.31 (10k primitives) will be exactly this shape. Prior art (GPUI, ImGui, WebRender) all trivially reject against the clip on the CPU.
- proposed change: Add R4.2a: "At submission the batcher MUST drop a draw whose transformed bounds (including shadow padding and ink extent) do not intersect the current clip rect, and MUST count it as `culled` (chapter 13, R13.13). For text the test is per run, not per glyph. An implementation MAY additionally clamp an axis-aligned, translate-only quad's vertices to the clip rect (moving the rect-local coordinate and UV by the same amount) so that clipped fragments are never rasterised; the fragment test remains as the general case."

#### G8. Keep no sentinel for "no clip" (minor)

- location: chapter 4, R4.1, R4.2
- claim: The clip stack has three states; while `empty` the batcher drops draws "or encode[s] a rect that discards every fragment".
- problem: R4.1 implies a special "no clip" encoding on the GPU (worldsim's `(0,0,0,0)`), which is the sentinel the empty-intersection bug came from, and it costs a branch per fragment. `none` and `empty` are both CPU concerns.
- proposed change: Append to R4.1: "There is no GPU-side 'no clip' value: a draw under `none` carries the current target's rect (or an all-covering rect such as (-1e8, -1e8, 1e8, 1e8)); a draw under `empty` is never uploaded. The fragment test is unconditional."

#### G9. Nested rounded clips have one per-draw slot; say what happens to the second (major)

- location: chapter 4, R4.14, R4.15
- claim: "A rounded-rectangle clip SHOULD be supported without breaking batching by carrying the clip's rounded-rect SDF parameters as per-draw data".
- problem: A rounded panel (R12.19) clipping with the rounded clip, containing a rounded scroll container (R12.20) that also clips, containing a rounded avatar mask, is three rounded clips on one glyph. One slot cannot express it; the intersection of two rounded rects is not a rounded rect. The chapter must define the degradation or the fallback, or implementers will pick different ones and the gallery goldens will disagree between the two reference implementations.
- proposed change: Append to R4.14: "A draw carries the axis-aligned intersection of every clip on the stack plus the SDF parameters of the innermost rounded clip only. Outer rounded clips contribute their bounding rect (documented approximation: content may show in the outer clip's corners by at most the outer radius). A development build warns once per frame when two rounded clips nest; an implementation with the stencil path (R4.15) MAY route the outer clip through it instead. The rounded edge is anti-aliased over one device pixel and multiplies coverage; its cost is one extra SDF per fragment for every draw inside the clip, text included, which is acceptable." Add to R12.19 and R12.20 that the inset-by-radius rule applies when a second rounded clip would nest.

#### G10. Rotated clips: the AABB under-clips; offer the oriented rect through the same slot (minor)

- location: chapter 4, R4.7
- claim: Under rotation "the implementation MUST either use the axis-aligned bounds of the transformed rect (documented approximation) or fall back to stencil clipping".
- problem: The AABB of a rotated rect is larger than the rect, so content leaks past the rotated container's edges (a scrolling list inside a tilted card shows rows outside the card). The stencil path costs two flushes per clip. A rotated rect is a two-parameter SDF (`sdBox` on `rotate(p - centre)`), the same mechanism and cost as R4.14's rounded clip, and it nests with rect clips by the same rule as G9.
- proposed change: Add to R4.7: "An implementation MAY carry an oriented-rectangle clip (centre, half size, rotation) in the per-draw clip slot of R4.14 and evaluate it in the fragment stage; the axis-aligned bounds then serve only the CPU cull of R4.2a. A development build MUST warn when a clip is pushed under a non-translate transform and neither the oriented clip nor the stencil path is implemented."

### Chapter 5, primitives and shading

#### G11. The border-over-fill formula is wrong at the outer edge and for translucent borders (blocker)

- location: chapter 5, R5.8, R5.23
- claim: "The border region blends over the fill: `mix(fill, border, borderBlend)` where `borderBlend` is the product of the inner and outer edge ramps." And R5.23: "final fragment alpha is `shapeCoverage * fill.a * opacity` (with the border mix applied to colour and alpha together)".
- problem: Two errors that both show as fringes. (a) At the outer edge of a bordered rect the pixel is part border and part background; `mix(fill, border, coverage * innerRamp)` then times coverage makes it part *fill*, part border, times coverage. For a dark border on a light fill every outer edge pixel is lightened by the fill colour: a visible pale halo one pixel wide around every bordered control, worse on dark themes with light borders (dark halo). The product formulation double-applies the outer ramp. (b) With straight-alpha `mix` of colour and alpha, a 50 percent alpha border over an opaque fill yields output alpha 0.5, so the *backdrop* shows through the border. CSS (`background-clip: border-box`, the default) paints the background under the border and the border over it, so a translucent border shows the fill through it and the result is opaque. GPUI's shader does exactly that (border composited over background, then mixed by the inner-edge coverage). The proposed formula is exact area coverage and has no product term.
- proposed change: Replace R5.8 with: "Let `co = clamp(0.5 - dOuter / w, 0, 1)` be the fraction of the pixel inside the outer edge and `ci = clamp(0.5 - dInner / w, 0, 1)` the fraction inside the inner edge, where `dOuter` and `dInner` are the signed distances to the outer and inner border edges (for `inside`: outer = shape edge, inner = shape edge inset by the border width; `center`: outset and inset by half the width; `outside`: outset by the width and the shape edge; the inner rounded box uses radius `max(0, r - inset)`), `w` is the device-pixel footprint of one unit of distance, and `ci <= co` always. With premultiplied `fillP` and `borderP`, and `borderRegionP = borderP + fillP * (1 - borderP.a)` (the border composited over the fill, CSS `background-clip: border-box`), the fragment output is `(ci * fillP + (co - ci) * borderRegionP) * opacity`. A zero-width border gives `ci == co` and reduces to `co * fillP`. A fully transparent fill with a visible border is legal and gives `(co - ci) * borderP`. Discard when `co < 1/1024`." Replace R5.23's formula with a reference to this rule. Add a pixel test to 5.10: "a 1 px opaque dark border on an opaque light fill, unsnapped by half a device pixel: no pixel of the outer edge row is lighter than the border colour blended over the clear colour by its coverage; a border with alpha 0.5 over an opaque fill produces opaque output".

#### G12. Quads must be inflated for the AA ramp, the AA ramp must be one pixel and linear, and shadow quads need three sigma (major)

- location: chapter 5, R5.6, R5.7, R5.11, R5.12; chapter 6, R6.5
- claim: R5.6: "the edge alpha ramp spans one device pixel of distance"; R5.7: only `center` and `outside` borders expand the quad; R5.11: the shadow quad is "grown again by `blur` on every side".
- problem: Three related geometry errors. (a) A one-pixel ramp centred on the edge puts half of it outside the geometric rect. A quad that is exactly the rect never rasterises the pixels whose centres lie up to half a device pixel outside the edge, so every fractional-positioned fill and every `inside` border is anti-aliased on the inner side only; edges look one-sided and animated shapes crawl. Skia inflates rrect bounds by a pixel for exactly this reason. (b) The chapter never writes the ramp down. Worldsim's shader uses `1 - smoothstep(-px, +px, d)`, a two-pixel S-curve; an implementer who copies it gets soft hairlines and text-adjacent borders that look out of focus next to the linear one-pixel ramp R6.5 implies for glyphs. The exact box-filter coverage of a straight edge is the linear ramp `clamp(0.5 - d / w, 0, 1)`, which is also what R5.6 itself recommends for the axis-aligned case, so make it the only formula. (c) With `sigma = blur / 2`, growing the shadow quad by `blur` truncates the Gaussian at two sigma, where a black 50 percent shadow still has about 1 percent alpha (2 to 3 of 255 levels): a visible straight cutoff line around large soft shadows on light backgrounds. Evan Wallace's shader pads by `3 * sigma`. The `1 - smoothstep(-blur, blur, dist)` approximation does reach zero at `blur`, which is why the two statements in R5.12 are only consistent with each other for that approximation.
- proposed change: R5.6: "Coverage of an edge is `clamp(0.5 - d / w, 0, 1)` with `d` the signed distance in the SDF's units and `w` the device-pixel footprint of one unit (`length(vec2(dFdx(d), dFdy(d)))`, or a per-draw constant when the transform is translate-only); `smoothstep` and ramps wider than one device pixel MUST NOT be used for shape or glyph edges." R5.7 (all positions, including plain fills): "The quad MUST extend at least one device pixel beyond the outermost SDF edge on every side (the shape edge, or the border's outward extent for `center` and `outside`), while the SDF parameters keep the shape's own size; the extra pixel is where the outer half of the ramp lives." R5.11 and R5.12: "The shadow quad is the spread shape padded by `3 * sigma` (1.5 times `blur`) on every side when the erf form is used, and by `blur` when the smoothstep approximation is used; the approximation SHOULD be limited to `blur <= 8` logical pixels." Add to 5.10: "a rect at x 10.25 has symmetric one-pixel ramps on its left and right edges".

#### G13. Shadow spread must grow the corner radii the CSS way, and the tail rule needs a source (minor)

- location: chapter 5, R5.11, R5.12
- claim: "grown by `spread`, then grown again by `blur`"; "`sigma = blur / 2` (the CSS `box-shadow` mapping)".
- problem: `sigma = blur / 2` is correct: CSS Backgrounds 3 requires a Gaussian "with a standard deviation equal to half the blur radius" within 5 percent per pixel (confirmed via the CSS WG issue record and David Baron's write-up, links in the verification log). Growing the shape by `spread` without growing its corner radius produces a shadow whose corners are visibly squarer than the owner's; CSS 6.1.1 adds the spread to each corner radius (with a smoothing formula for radii smaller than the spread, `spread * (1 + (r / spread - 1)^3)`), which is what makes a spread shadow read as "the same shape, bigger".
- proposed change: Append to R5.11: "The spread shape's corner radii are the owner's radii plus `spread` (CSS Backgrounds 3, section 6.1.1; for a radius smaller than the spread use `r + spread * (1 + (r / spread - 1)^3)`); negative spread shrinks radii the same way, clamped at zero." Keep R5.12's mapping and cite the specification section rather than "the CSS mapping".

#### G14. Gradients must interpolate premultiplied colour (minor)

- location: chapter 5, R5.9, R5.22
- claim: "Fill MAY be a per-corner colour (four colours, interpolated by the rasteriser)"; premultiplication happens "either on the CPU when packing vertices or as the last step of the fragment stage".
- problem: Interpolating straight colours and premultiplying afterwards passes a transparent-to-red gradient through dark red at the midpoint (the classic "gray in the middle of transparent to white"). CSS gradients interpolate in premultiplied space; the two reference implementations will disagree on every fade-out gradient unless the rule picks one.
- proposed change: Append to R5.9: "Per-corner colours are premultiplied before interpolation (on the CPU when packing, or per vertex in the vertex stage), never after, so a gradient to transparent does not darken at its midpoint (CSS gradient semantics)."

#### G15. The 64-byte instance cannot hold the fields the chapter requires; give a layout or a side table (major)

- location: chapter 5, R5.3, R5.4, R5.7a, R5.9; chapter 4, R4.14; chapter 15, 15.11 table ("a 64-byte instance uses 4" attributes)
- claim: "an instanced layout of about 64 bytes per instance with a shared unit quad".
- problem: Adding up what R5.3 and the rules around it require per instance: rect (4 f32, 16 bytes; half floats lose sub-pixel precision above 2048), clip rect (16), four corner radii (8 as half floats), fill and border RGBA8 (8), border width, position, mode, texture slot, opacity (about 8), UV rect (8 as normalised u16; half floats have only about 1/2048 resolution, two texels on a 4096 atlas), four gradient colours (16), shadow blur and spread (4), rounded-clip rect and radius (about 20). That is 100 to 120 bytes inline. And attribute slots are counted per declared attribute, not per 16 bytes: a mixed-type 64-byte instance packs into about nine attributes (float rect, unorm16 uv, half radii, unorm8 fill, unorm8 border, half widths, an integer `uvec4` via `vertexAttribIPointer`, indices), which is fine against the 16 limit but not "4".
- proposed change: Replace the size sentence of R5.4 with a normative-shape, non-normative-size statement and a worked layout: "Per-instance data SHOULD be packed with normalised 8-bit colours, 16-bit normalised UVs, and half-float radii and widths (all core WebGL2 attribute types); values that are shared by many draws in a flush (clip rect and rounded-clip parameters, gradient corner colours) SHOULD be stored once per flush in a uniform block or data texture and referenced by a 16-bit index per instance, fetched in the vertex stage and passed as flat varyings. A worked 64-byte layout: rect f32x4 (16), uv unorm16x4 (8), radii f16x4 (8), fill rgba8 (4), border rgba8 (4), borderWidth f16 and blur f16 (4), mode u8, borderPosition u8, textureSlot u8, flags u8 (4), clipIndex u16 and gradientIndex u16 (4), 12 bytes spare. Inline clips and gradients cost about 96 to 112 bytes and are also acceptable." Fix the 15.11 row to "MAX_VERTEX_ATTRIBS 16 (about 9 used by the packed instance layout)". Also note in R5.4 that "32-bit indices MUST" applies to the indexed vertex-quad path only; the instanced path has a six-index unit quad or none.

#### G16. "One GPU draw per texture switch" is only realistic with permanently bound atlases and texture-agnostic shape draws (major, performance)

- location: chapter 5, R5.20, R5.21; chapter 2, 2.8 example; chapter 6, R6.4; chapter 13, R13.13
- claim: A texture change "splits the GPU submission at that point in emit order"; multi-texture selection is optional; the worked example promises "one GPU draw if the mono atlas is already bound".
- problem: With a single bound texture and naive splitting, a list of 40 rows each drawing an icon (icon atlas), a label (body atlas), and a mono value (mono atlas) is 120 GPU draws, and a card with art (art atlas) plus two text roles is four. The spec's three font roles plus the icon atlas plus one UI art atlas are five textures that every screen uses in an interleaved order; the number of splits is proportional to content, exactly what R3.24 says barriers must not be. Untextured shapes must also never force a split (worldsim tags them 0 and lets them ride whatever is bound); the chapter does not say so.
- proposed change: Replace the last sentence of R5.20 with: "Shape and text-free draws (`flat`, `rect`, `shadow`, `circle`) are texture-agnostic and never split a submission. The implementation MUST bind a fixed set of resident textures to fixed units for the whole frame (the three font-role atlases, the icon atlas, and the UI art atlas at minimum; a `TEXTURE_2D_ARRAY` of card-art pages counts as one unit) and select per draw by a texture-slot index in a `switch` (dynamic sampler indexing is not permitted in GLSL ES 3.00). A submission splits only when a draw references a texture that is not in the current unit set and every dynamic unit is taken (`textureSlotsExhausted`), never merely because consecutive draws use different resident textures. The batch test of 5.10 becomes: a screen of shapes, text in three roles, icons, and images from the UI atlas is one GPU draw per flush."

#### G17. Buffer ring sizing and the per-flush versus per-frame uniform block contradiction (major)

- location: chapter 5, R5.27, R5.28; chapter 3, R3.24; chapter 15, R15.11, R15.14, R15.15
- claim: R5.27: "a ring of at least two buffers (three SHOULD be the default), so a mid-frame flush never rewrites a buffer the GPU is still reading". R5.28: per-flush uniforms are the projection and friends. R15.15: uniform state "lives in one `std140` uniform block written once per frame".
- problem: A ring of three buffers rotated per flush is exhausted by a frame with three flushes (world, world UI, screen UI is already three; each opacity composite of G2 adds one): the fourth flush of frame N writes the buffer flush one of the same frame is still reading. The ring must cover flushes per frame times frames in flight, or use one large buffer with an advancing write offset that wraps only every N frames. With instanced quads, each ring slot needs its own VAO (R15.14) or a re-pointing of attribute offsets. And R5.28 says the projection changes per flush (it must, for off-screen targets and the world-space UI domain) while R15.15 says the block is written once per frame: both cannot hold; the WebGL rule that every active uniform block must be fully backed makes a wrong offset a draw error, not a wrong picture.
- proposed change: R5.27: "Vertex, index, and instance storage on the GPU is either a ring of at least `3 * flushesPerFrame` fixed-capacity buffers, or one buffer per stream of capacity at least three frames of data written at a monotonically advancing offset that wraps only when the region being overwritten is at least two frames old; both need one VAO per buffer (or per slot) bound once per flush. `bufferSubData` into a region the GPU may still read is prohibited." R15.15: "one 256-byte-aligned uniform-block slot per flush in a ring of the same depth, written with one `bufferSubData` per flush (or all slots at once when the flush sequence is known at frame start) and selected with `bindBufferRange`; the projection, target size, and sampler slots are per flush, nothing is per draw."

#### G18. Polygon feathering and abutting SDF quads produce seams; say so and snap (minor)

- location: chapter 5, R5.10, R5.17; chapter 7, R7.8
- claim: R5.17: "the implementation SHOULD add a one-pixel feathered outline on the CPU for the polygon and triangle components"; R5.10: a radius-0 rect goes through the SDF path "so its edges anti-alias identically to its rounded neighbours".
- problem: Two adjacent rects sharing a fractional edge each cover the shared pixel column at 50 percent; `over` composites them to 75 percent, a visible light hairline between every pair of abutting cells in a table, a segmented control, or a progress bar drawn as two rects. Feathered polygons that share an edge (a tessellated icon made of several fans) show the same seam, now one pixel wide. This is the conflation artifact every analytic-coverage renderer has; it is not mentioned anywhere.
- proposed change: Append to R5.10: "Abutting SDF edges conflate (two 50 percent coverages compose to 75 percent, a light seam). Components that tile rects edge to edge (tables, segmented controls, split bars, tab strips) MUST snap the shared edges to the device grid under translate-only transforms (chapter 7, R7.8a) or draw one rect with per-corner colours instead of two; the fixture of 5.10 includes two abutting rects at a fractional x and expects no seam." Append to R5.17: "The feather ring is one device pixel wide (`1 / ratio` logical), its outer vertices carry the polygon colour with alpha 0 (premultiplied, so zero), its inner vertices the polygon colour, drawn in `flat` mode after the polygon; polygons that share edges MUST be feathered as one outline, not per triangle."

### Chapter 6, text

#### G19. Pixel range 4 at 32 px fails the chapter's own minimum below 16 px at ratio 1; small text needs a rule (major)

- location: chapter 6, R6.1, R6.4a, R6.5, 6.9 fixture (10, 12, 13 px at dpr 1)
- claim: "generate at 32 px per em with a pixel range of 4 (the range must stay above about 2 screen pixels at the smallest rendered size or the anti-aliasing collapses)".
- problem: The screen-space range is `pxrange * size / em = size / 8`: 2.0 px at 16 px, 1.6 at 13, 1.5 at 12, 1.25 at 10, at ratio 1. msdfgen's README (fetched, see log) says `screenPxRange()` "must never be lower than 1" and "if it is lower than 2, there is a high probability that the anti-aliasing will fail", and its reference shader clamps with `max(..., 1.0)`. The chapter's numbers therefore violate its own constraint for every token size under 16 px on a 100 percent Windows desktop, which is where card body text lives. Two further facts the chapter does not state: MSDF has no hinting, so 10 to 12 px text at ratio 1 renders lighter and less even than DirectWrite or CoreText output of the same face, and a 32 px em sampled at 10 px is 0.31x minification of a three-channel field, which produces uneven stems and occasional corner blobs (the median of three bilinearly minified channels is not a distance). R6.1 already permits per-(face, size, ratio) rasters; the chapter never says when to use them. At ratio 2 all of this is moot (10 px is 20 device px, range 2.5), which is why it has not been noticed on a Mac.
- proposed change: Replace R6.4a with: "Atlas parameters: the ratio `pxrange / emSize` MUST be at least 1/6 so that the screen-space range is at least 2 device pixels at 12 logical px and ratio 1 (for example 48 px per em with `-pxrange 8`, or 32 with 6); the shader clamps the computed range to a minimum of 1 as in msdfgen's reference shader. `mtsdf` SHOULD be used. Sizes whose screen-space range would fall below 1.5 device pixels at the current ratio (below about 9 logical px at ratio 1 with the parameters above) SHOULD be rendered from a rasterised per-(face, size, ratio) atlas built by the platform's 2D text API at load time (R6.1's permitted alternative), in which case measurement still follows R6.8 using the MSDF metrics scaled by `size / em`, so layout does not change when the renderer switches path. The visual fixture at ratio 1 is a scored gate, not a demonstration: the 10, 12, and 13 px rows MUST be compared against a platform-rasterised reference for stem weight and evenness, and the token scale SHOULD not define a text size below 11 logical px."

#### G20. Align the glyph coverage formula with msdfgen and with R5.6 (minor)

- location: chapter 6, R6.5
- claim: "applies `smoothstep(-0.5, 0.5, distance)` as coverage".
- problem: msdfgen's shader is the linear `clamp(screenPxDistance + 0.5, 0, 1)`; G12 makes the linear one-pixel ramp normative for shapes. Using an S-curve for glyphs and a linear ramp for shapes gives text edges a different softness from the border one pixel away. The chapter also does not say how `mtsdf`'s alpha channel is used (body from the RGB median, effects from alpha) or that the range may be a per-draw constant under translate-only transforms, which avoids `fwidth` on the UV entirely.
- proposed change: R6.5: "Coverage is `clamp(screenPxRange * (median(r, g, b) - 0.5) + 0.5, 0, 1)` with `screenPxRange = max(pxrange * size * ratio / emSize, 1)`; under translate-only transforms it is a per-draw constant, otherwise derived from `fwidth(uv)` as in msdfgen's reference shader. The `mtsdf` alpha channel is a true SDF used for outlines, glows, and shadows; the body uses the median. Coverage multiplies premultiplied colour and alpha together."

#### G21. The run-snapping rationale is wrong, though the rule is right (minor)

- location: chapter 6, R6.16, 6.9 "Snap" test
- claim: "Every glyph in the run moves by the same delta, so kerning and advances are untouched and the distance field is sampled on-grid; fractional run origins blur MSDF edges visibly."
- problem: Only the first glyph lands on the grid; every following glyph sits at a fractional advance, and a distance field does not need texel alignment anyway (its edge is analytic). What snapping the origin buys is (a) a baseline on a whole device row, so ascenders and x-heights of neighbouring labels align and stems have the same weight from frame to frame, and (b) no shimmer when a run's origin animates sub-pixel. Stating the wrong reason invites someone to "fix" it by snapping every glyph, which changes measured widths.
- proposed change: R6.16 rationale sentence: "Snapping the origin puts the baseline on a device row and keeps a run's stems at the same sub-pixel phase from frame to frame, which is what stops shimmer and weight flicker; it is not about texel alignment. Per-glyph snapping MUST NOT change advances; an implementation MAY snap each glyph's x origin independently at sizes under 16 px and ratio 1 for stem consistency, provided the run's total width and R6.8 are unchanged."

#### G22. `Intl.Segmenter` does not do line breaking; define the break opportunities (major)

- location: chapter 6, R6.13
- claim: "an implementation SHOULD use the platform's segmenter (`Intl.Segmenter` in a browser, UAX #14 rules elsewhere) so that scripts without spaces wrap correctly".
- problem: `Intl.Segmenter` offers `grapheme`, `word`, and `sentence` granularities only (MDN, fetched); word segmentation is UAX #29, not UAX #14 line breaking. Using it as the break source gives wrong answers in both directions: a no-break space (U+00A0) is a word boundary and would break; a hyphen and a zero-width space (U+200B) are legal break opportunities that "spaces in the baseline" never finds; CJK word segmentation is dictionary based and forbids breaks inside multi-character words that kinsoku rules allow. The two reference implementations will wrap the same string differently.
- proposed change: R6.13: "Break opportunities in the baseline are: after U+0020 and U+0009; after U+200B; after U+002D and U+2010 when followed by a letter or digit; never at U+00A0, U+202F, U+2011, U+2060, or U+FEFF; a hard newline always breaks. For runs of scripts without spaces (Han, Hiragana, Katakana, Thai) an implementation SHOULD allow a break between grapheme clusters (`Intl.Segmenter` with `granularity: 'grapheme'`, or UAX #29 elsewhere); full UAX #14 and kinsoku rules are optional. `Intl.Segmenter` is a grapheme and word segmenter, not a line breaker, and MUST NOT be used as the sole source of break opportunities." Add wrap tests for U+00A0 (no break) and U+200B (break).

#### G23. Emoji fallback must still feed measurement and the ratio (minor)

- location: chapter 6, R6.4c, R6.8; chapter 15, R15.19
- claim: Platform-rasterised runs "are measured by the platform and do not participate in R6.8's shared iteration".
- problem: A string mixing atlas glyphs and one emoji still has to satisfy measure-equals-render, so the platform-measured advance must enter the shared iteration as the advance of that run; the sprite must be rasterised at `size * ratio` and re-rasterised on a ratio change; the sprite page must be uploaded premultiplied with colour-space conversion off (the opposite of R6.4b's distance atlases); and glyph quads for such runs are `image` draws that split the submission unless the sprite page is one of the resident units (G16).
- proposed change: R6.4c: "The fallback rasterises the run through the platform's 2D text API at `size * ratio` into a premultiplied RGBA sprite page (uploaded with `UNPACK_PREMULTIPLY_ALPHA_WEBGL` true and colour-space conversion off, or via `createImageBitmap` with `premultiplyAlpha: 'premultiply'` and `colorSpaceConversion: 'none'`), keyed on (text, face, size, ratio), and draws it as an `image` quad from a resident unit; the run's platform-measured advance enters the shared iteration of R6.8 as that run's width so measurement and rendering agree for mixed strings."

### Chapter 7, coordinates and ratio

#### G24. The hairline test contradicts the rule, and the rule must snap edges and the border width (major)

- location: chapter 7, R7.8, 7.8 test "Hairline snap"; chapter 4 (clip rects)
- claim: R7.8: "Snapping moves the whole rect by less than half a device pixel and never changes its size by more than one device pixel." Test: "a 1 px border at y 10.4 covers device row 21 fully at dpr 2 and no part of rows 20 or 22."
- problem: A 1 logical-pixel border at ratio 2 is two device rows (20.8 to 22.8, snapped 21 to 23, rows 21 and 22). The test asserts a one-device-row line, which is half the specified thickness; an implementation that passes the test draws every hairline at half weight on high-density screens, and one that draws the correct weight fails the test. The rule is also ambiguous: moving the whole rect keeps a fractional far edge (still blurry), while "size changes by at most one pixel" implies snapping each edge. Nothing says the border width itself snaps, and nothing says a scroll container's clip rect snaps (G6 makes the clip edge hard, so an unsnapped clip shimmers).
- proposed change: R7.8: "Under translate-only transforms, an axis-aligned rect whose border width is at or below 1 logical px SHOULD snap each of its four edges independently to the device grid (`round(edge * ratio) / ratio`) and its border width to `max(1, round(width * ratio)) / ratio`; each edge moves by less than half a device pixel, the size changes by at most one device pixel per axis, and the border occupies whole device pixels." Add R7.8a: "Clip rects of containers, and the shared edges of abutting rects (chapter 5, R5.10), are snapped the same way." Fix the test: "a 1 px border at y 10.4 with ratio 2 covers device rows 21 and 22 fully and no part of rows 20 or 23; with ratio 1 it covers row 10 fully."

#### G25. With `uiScale` the ratio everything snaps to is `dpr * uiScale`; input conversion order is reversed (minor)

- location: chapter 7, R7.2, R7.5, R7.14; chapter 4, R4.4; chapter 6, R6.16
- claim: `dpr` is the ratio used by the clip test and snapping; `uiScale` "is applied by defining the logical viewport as `framebuffer / (dpr * uiScale)`"; input positions are converted "dividing by `uiScale` and subtracting the canvas origin".
- problem: Once the logical viewport is `framebuffer / (dpr * uiScale)`, one logical pixel is `dpr * uiScale` device pixels, so every rule that snaps or tests against "the device grid" using `dpr` alone is wrong by the scale factor (text origins land off-grid at `uiScale` 1.25). And the input sentence has the operations in the wrong order: the canvas origin is in CSS pixels and must be subtracted before dividing.
- proposed change: Define once in 7.1: "`ratio` (device pixels per logical pixel) is `dpr * uiScale`; it, not `dpr`, is the value chapters 4, 5, 6, and 7 snap and test against and the value the mount context exposes as the viewport's ratio; `dpr` alone is used only to size the canvas backing store." R7.14: "converted once at the dispatcher's entry: subtract the canvas origin (CSS pixels), then divide by `uiScale`".

#### G26. The ratio reaches more places than the three named (nit)

- location: chapter 7, intro and R7.2
- claim: "The device pixel ratio exists in exactly three places: the framebuffer size, the fragment-stage clip test, and pixel snapping."
- problem: It also sets the polygon feather width (R5.17, one device pixel), the shadow padding and AA footprint when computed per draw (G12), the MSDF screen range when computed per draw (G20), the emoji raster size (G23), and the rounded-clip edge ramp. With G6 it leaves the clip test.
- proposed change: "The ratio is consumed by the viewport, by pixel snapping, by the per-draw device-pixel constants the batcher computes at submission (AA footprint, feather width, shadow padding, MSDF range, fallback raster size), and by screenshots. It is never read by layout, components, or the tree walk."

### Chapters 13 and 15, observability and the WebGL2 backend

#### G27. One `TIME_ELAPSED` query around the whole frame measures GPU wall time including bubbles, and the disjoint read contradicts R15.22 (major)

- location: chapter 13, R13.16 to R13.18; chapter 15, R15.22, R15.24
- claim: "time the whole frame's GPU work, from the first clear to the last flush inclusive"; "One `TIME_ELAPSED_EXT` query per frame around the whole frame"; and R15.22: "No `getError`, `getParameter`, ... inside the frame loop."
- problem: `TIME_ELAPSED` measures the GPU timeline between the begin and end markers, so with several flushes per frame it includes every gap in which the GPU sat idle waiting for the CPU to submit the next flush. A CPU-bound UI frame (the common case: layout plus text plus a 10k-group upload) reports a GPU time close to the CPU frame time, which is the misleading number chapter 13 exists to prevent. Queries cannot nest but may run back to back; one query per flush, summed, approximates GPU busy time and also gives per-pass attribution, which is what worldsim's "the timer's scope excluded the final flush" incident really wanted. Separately, reading a result needs `getQueryParameter(q, QUERY_RESULT_AVAILABLE)` and `getParameter(GPU_DISJOINT_EXT)` (the extension's own sample code, local copy), and MDN lists every `get*Parameter` as a "possible flush + round-trip"; R15.22 prohibits exactly these in the frame loop. The chapter needs the exception spelled out or the GPU timer is non-conforming by construction.
- proposed change: R13.16: "Where timer queries exist, the implementation SHOULD issue one `TIME_ELAPSED` query per GPU submission pass (each flush, each off-screen composite, the clear), never nested, and report their sum as `gpu.ms` and the per-pass values under `gpu.passes`; a single query spanning the frame MAY be reported additionally as `gpu.spanMs` and is documented as including CPU-induced idle time." R15.22 (append): "The only synchronous entry points permitted in the frame loop are `getQueryParameter` with `QUERY_RESULT_AVAILABLE` or `QUERY_RESULT`, `getParameter(GPU_DISJOINT_EXT)`, and `clientWaitSync` with timeout 0, at most once per pending query per frame, and only in development builds with the GPU timer enabled; production builds issue none."

#### G28. The fence pattern is missing its flush, and the fallback measures latency, not GPU time (minor)

- location: chapter 15, R15.23, R15.24; chapter 13, R13.19, snapshot schema `gpu`
- claim: "Fences (`fenceSync` after the frame, `clientWaitSync` with timeout 0 polled on later frames) are the only synchronisation"; "The fence pattern of R15.23 provides 'GPU finished frame N at CPU time T' as a latency measure where the timer is absent."
- problem: Two omissions. The WebGL 2.0 specification (local copy, "In order to ensure consistent behavior across platforms") says sync objects only transition to signaled when the event loop is not executing a task, and MDN's async-readback example calls `gl.flush()` immediately after `fenceSync` because an unflushed fence may never reach the GPU while the page idles; an implementation that polls a fence issued after the last draw of a frame without flushing can wait forever. And the fallback number is frame latency; if it lands in `gpu.ms` the dashboard shows a "GPU time" that is really presentation latency, the exact class of unsanity-checked metric R13.5 forbids.
- proposed change: R15.23: "After `fenceSync(SYNC_GPU_COMMANDS_COMPLETE, 0)` the implementation MUST call `flush()` (or pass `SYNC_FLUSH_COMMANDS_BIT` on the first `clientWaitSync`); a fence never signals within the task that created it, so polling starts on the next frame with `clientWaitSync(sync, 0, 0)` or `getSyncParameter(sync, SYNC_STATUS)`." R13.19: "The fence fallback is reported as `gpu.latencyMs` (CPU time from submission to the fence signal), never as `gpu.ms`; `gpu.ms` is `null` when no timer query exists."

#### G29. Flush reasons list impossible events and omit real ones; `drawCalls` means two different things (minor)

- location: chapter 13, R13.12, R13.13; chapter 2, R2.16; chapter 3, 3.1 "Draw call"
- claim: R13.13: reasons "MUST include at least: `textureChange`, `clipChange`, `blendChange`, `shaderChange`, `bufferFull`, `barrier`, `endFrame`". R13.12: `drawCalls` per frame. Chapter 3 defines a draw call as "one call on the immediate draw API"; R2.16 has foreign draws report "their draw calls" (GPU draws); worldsim's "3 draw calls for the whole game" counted GPU submissions.
- problem: Chapters 3, 4, and 5 say clip, shader, and blend changes never flush, so three of the seven mandatory reasons are events a conforming implementation cannot produce; the list is either a bug detector (then say so) or dead. Missing are the splits that do happen under this design: texture slot exhaustion (G16), render-target switches (G2), stencil levels (R4.15), draws culled by clip (G7), groups occluded (G4), and groups per layer, which is the number that explains a sort. `drawCalls` is used for API calls in one chapter and GPU draws in another, so the two reference implementations will report different things under one name.
- proposed change: R13.12: "`apiDraws` (draw-API calls, equal to draw groups plus shadow groups), `gpuDraws` (`drawElements`, `drawArrays`, and instanced variants, foreign passes included), `vertices`, `triangles`, `instances`, `culled`, `occluded`." R13.13: "`flushes` by reason: `barrier`, `endFrame`, `targetChange`, `bufferFull`; `splits` (GPU draws within a flush beyond the first) by reason: `textureSlotsExhausted`, `blendChange`, `stencilLevel`; plus `groupsByLayer[]` and `reorderedGroups`. `clipChange` and `shaderChange` are retained as counters that MUST read zero; a non-zero value fails the batch test of chapter 5."

#### G30. Derivatives in non-uniform control flow are undefined, not zero (minor)

- location: chapter 15, R15.8
- claim: "Derivatives (`dFdx`, `dFdy`, `fwidth`) are core; they return zero inside non-uniform control flow".
- problem: GLSL ES 3.00 section 8.8 (local copy of the specification text): "Derivatives are undefined within non-uniform control flow." The sentence about returning 0 is in section 4.3.3 and concerns derivatives "evaluated inside an initializer with an argument that is a constant expression", a different thing. "Undefined" is worse than zero: ANGLE's translators and the underlying D3D or Metal compilers may produce garbage or a compile failure, not a quiet zero. The advice in the rule (evaluate before branching) is right; the justification is wrong and an implementer relying on "zero" will write a shader that reads the derivative inside the mode switch as a safe no-op.
- proposed change: R15.8: "Derivatives are core in GLSL ES 3.00 but undefined within non-uniform control flow (section 8.8). Compute every derivative-dependent quantity (the AA footprint, the MSDF range) before the `switch` on the primitive mode and pass it in; a per-instance branch is uniform across a 2x2 fragment quad in practice, but the specification does not promise it."

#### G31. Version and platform claims to correct (minor)

- location: chapter 13, mapping table "Runtime floor" and "Frame sections"; chapter 15, R15.4, R15.24, R15.33, R15.42; 15.11 table
- claim: (a) "Chromium 128 or newer for the DevTools hooks (... the six-argument `console.timeStamp` 128)". (b) "Chromium removed the automatic SwiftShader fallback in version 137". (c) `ResizeObserver` with `device-pixel-content-box` "where available". (d) Safari timer queries "a feature flag"; `WEBGL_multi_draw` "Chromium and Safari only".
- problem: (a) The six-argument `console.timeStamp` form landed with Chrome 134's DevTools (Chrome DevTools release notes; the spec's own research report 05, section D.5, says 134), and the extensibility page states no minimum version; `performance.measure` with `detail.devtools` is the older path. 128 is wrong and would make a feature-detect on `console.timeStamp.length` pass on a runtime that draws nothing. (b) The Chromium SwiftShader document (fetched) states the fallback "has been deprecated and WebGL context creation will soon fail" without a version; the Intent to Remove estimated desktop 137 with experiments continuing in 139, so "removed in 137" overstates a rollout; the flags are right. (c) `device-pixel-content-box` is Chromium and Firefox; WebKit never implemented it (web.dev and the CSS WG list, links in the log), so the fallback path is the normal path on Safari and must be tested, not treated as an edge. (d) Both Safari statements match the last published data (opt-in since Safari 17.0/17.1; Firefox still lacks `WEBGL_multi_draw` on caniuse) but the chapter should carry a verification date, since these flip.
- proposed change: R15.42 and the table: "Chromium 134 or newer for the six-argument `console.timeStamp`; `long-animation-frame` needs 123; older runtimes use `performance.measure` with `detail.devtools` (Chromium 128) or report `null`." R15.33: "Chromium has deprecated the automatic SwiftShader fallback (removal rolled out from 137); CI MUST pass the flags regardless of version." R15.4: "`device-pixel-content-box` (Chromium, Firefox; not WebKit), else `Math.round(clientSize * dpr)`, which is the normal path on Safari and is covered by the resize test." Add "verified 2026-09" to the 15.11 table rows for timer queries and `WEBGL_multi_draw`.

#### G32. Drags need pointer capture on the canvas (minor)

- location: chapter 15, R15.39; chapter 9, R9.12 (drag service)
- claim: The adapter listens to Pointer Events on the canvas with `touch-action: none`.
- problem: Without `setPointerCapture`, a drag that leaves the canvas (or the window, in a windowed Electron build) stops receiving `pointermove` and never receives `pointerup`; the drag ghost freezes and the button stays pressed. MDN (fetched): after `setPointerCapture`, "subsequent events for the pointer will be targeted at the capture element even when the pointer moves outside of its bounds" until `releasePointerCapture` or `pointerup`. The spec's own capture model (chapter 9) is only as good as the platform capture underneath it.
- proposed change: Append to R15.39: "On `pointerdown` the adapter calls `canvas.setPointerCapture(pointerId)` and releases it on `pointerup` or `pointercancel`, so moves and the release outside the canvas still arrive; `pointercancel` is delivered to the dispatcher as a cancel (chapter 9). The adapter calls `preventDefault` on `contextmenu` and on `wheel` (registered non-passive) and sets `user-select: none` on the canvas."

### Missing rules (chapters 2, 5, 15)

#### G34. Blend modes beyond `over`: additive costs nothing under premultiplication; the rest need a per-group blend key (major)

- location: chapter 5, R5.22; chapter 3, R3.3, R3.20; chapter 13, R13.13
- claim: "Blending is premultiplied `over` ... Every draw composites over whatever is already in the framebuffer."
- problem: A card game's UI wants additive glows and particles behind cards, multiply for shadows on art, and screen for highlights; under the current text every such effect is a foreign pass with a barrier, which breaks the "one domain, one sort" story for the most common visual effect. Under premultiplied `ONE, ONE_MINUS_SRC_ALPHA`, additive needs no state change at all: a fragment that outputs `(rgb * a, 0)` adds its colour and leaves the destination alpha, so an `additive` flag is per-draw data like everything else. Multiply and screen do need `blendFunc` changes and therefore a submission split.
- proposed change: Add R5.22a: "Each draw carries a blend mode: `over` (default) and `additive` are free, since `additive` is `over` with the output alpha forced to zero and no state change; `multiply` (`DST_COLOR, ONE_MINUS_SRC_ALPHA`) and `screen` (`ONE, ONE_MINUS_SRC_COLOR`) are per-group keys that split the GPU submission at that point in emit order without reordering, counted as `blendChange` (chapter 13, R13.13). Blend mode never affects sort order." Add the four modes to the 5.10 fixture over a photograph.

#### G35. Render targets, resource lifetime, card-art residency, and upload budgets are unspecified (major)

- location: chapter 2, R2.17; chapter 5, 5.5; chapter 7, R7.13; chapter 15, R15.5, R15.18 to R15.20; chapter 12, R12.5
- claim: Textures are created through the API and referenced by handle; uploads happen "outside the frame or at `beginFrame`"; atlases are 4096 and grow through texture arrays; UI textures are `LINEAR` without mipmaps "unless they are drawn at less than half size".
- problem: For a deckbuilder the bulk of GPU memory is card art: a few hundred images that do not fit one atlas, arrive over time, are drawn at 0.3 to 0.5x in the hand and at 0.15x in deck lists (so they need mipmaps, exactly the case R15.19 waves at), and must be decoded without stalling the frame. The chapters say nothing about who packs them, when they are evicted, how many bytes may be uploaded per frame (one 2048x2048 upload at `beginFrame` is a 4 ms hitch on ANGLE), what a component draws while art is not resident (R12.5 mentions a placeholder), whether context-loss recovery keeps CPU copies (doubling memory) or re-decodes, or how off-screen targets (G2, R7.13, R8.20) are owned and pooled.
- proposed change: Add a section 5.8a "Resources": "(a) Textures and targets are owned by a resource layer below the batcher with explicit `create`, `destroy`, and reference counts; components hold handles; the null backend counts them. (b) Card and image art is packed at runtime into a `TEXTURE_2D_ARRAY` of 2048 or 4096 pages (`texStorage3D` with a full mip chain, `LINEAR_MIPMAP_LINEAR`) by a shelf packer; pages are one texture unit; art drawn below 0.5x uses mipmaps; entries are evicted least-recently-drawn when a residency budget (default 256 MB, configurable) is exceeded. (c) Decoding uses `createImageBitmap` (`premultiplyAlpha: 'premultiply'`, `colorSpaceConversion: 'none'`) off the main thread where available; uploads are metered by a per-frame byte budget (default 2 MB) at `beginFrame`, oldest request first; a component whose texture is not yet resident draws its placeholder and re-renders when it becomes resident (chapter 12, R12.5). (d) On context loss the resource layer re-decodes from source or from CPU copies it was told to keep; it MUST NOT keep CPU copies of art by default. (e) Off-screen targets are pooled by size bucket, at most a configurable count, and released when unused for N frames. (f) `bytesUploaded`, `residentTextureBytes`, `pendingUploads`, and `evictions` join the counters of chapter 13."

#### G36. The options-object API and per-frame allocation; per-instance transforms (minor)

- location: chapter 2, R2.4, R2.8 to R2.13; chapter 5, R5.4, R5.26
- claim: R5.26: "zero per-frame allocation in steady state." R2.4: "Vertices are transformed on the CPU at submission." R2.8: `drawRect({ rect, fill, border, gradient, shadow, id })`.
- problem: Every draw call allocates an options object (and nested `border`, `shadow`, `rect` objects) in JavaScript; at 10k calls per frame that is 10k to 40k short-lived objects, which is usually absorbed by the young generation but is not "zero allocation", and R2.20's optional draw-list recording multiplies it. With instanced quads (R5.4 allows them) the CPU cannot pre-transform four vertices; a rotated instance needs a per-instance 2x3 transform (24 bytes, see G15) or must fall back to the vertex-quad path, and the chapter does not say which.
- proposed change: R5.26: "... zero per-frame allocation in steady state inside the batcher; the draw API's argument objects are the caller's and MAY be reused across frames (components SHOULD keep their style objects and pass them by reference); draw-list recording is development-only." R2.4 (append): "An instanced implementation carries translation and scale per instance and MUST route rotated draws through a per-instance 2x3 transform or through the vertex-quad path; either way the rect-local SDF coordinates are unaffected."

#### G37. Multi-window and colour space: two sentences would close them (nit)

- location: chapter 1, 1.6 non-goals; chapter 15, R15.2, R15.21
- claim: Nothing on either.
- problem: An Electron dashboard window (chapter 13 mentions "a second window") is a second GL context; textures and buffers do not cross contexts, so it needs its own backend, resource layer, and mount context. Wide-gamut displays are common on Macs; WebGL exposes `drawingBufferColorSpace`, and leaving it implicit means token colours may be reinterpreted as Display P3 on some future default.
- proposed change: 1.6: "Multiple windows: each window is a separate platform shell with its own backend, resource layer, and mount context; nothing GPU-side is shared." R15.2 (append): "`drawingBufferColorSpace` is set to `'srgb'` explicitly; HDR and wide-gamut output are non-goals."

## What is right and should not be changed

- The ordering model of chapter 3 is sound for the cases it names and for the ones asked in review: nested popups (child of the menu item, same layer, later in submission order), two modals (overlay roots in open order, second scrim covers the first), a drag ghost over a tooltip (`drag` above `tooltip`, acceptable since tooltips dismiss on press), a card overlapping a sibling panel (local `zIndex` or `raised`). The only real gaps are G1's monotonic effective layer and the host-already-high case.
- R3.16 shadow adjacency survives a texture split: the split is a submission detail in emit order and never reorders, and shadow and fill are both texture-agnostic (G16 makes that explicit); a stable partition by layer keeps them adjacent. No change needed beyond G3's wording.
- Component `zIndex` never reaching the batcher, the layer ordinal as the only batch key, explicit `flush()` as the only barrier, and the UI as the last content domain: correct, and the incident list justifies each.
- Clipping as per-draw data with a three-state stack, content offset before clip, and clip captured at submission: keep. G6 changes only where the test's coordinates come from.
- One uber shader with an explicit `flat` mode (the `smoothstep(0, 0, x)` hazard is real: GLSL ES 3.00 says "Results are undefined if edge0 >= edge1"), SDF rounded rects with clamped radii, per-corner radii, SDF circles and capsule lines, premultiplied `over` end to end, sRGB-space blending with no sRGB framebuffer, no MSAA, no depth buffer, 32-bit indices on the indexed path, typed reusable CPU arrays: all right.
- `sigma = blur / 2` matches CSS; the erf form as the preferred shadow; glow as a spread shadow; text shadow as a preceding run.
- MSDF over per-size rasters as the primary glyph source, `mtsdf`, msdf-atlas-gen's JSON as the one loader schema with validation, raw (non-premultiplied, no colour-space conversion) atlas uploads, measure-equals-render through one iteration, ascent-based vertical centring, no LCD filtering, run-origin snapping (with G21's corrected reason).
- Logical pixels everywhere public, `uiScale` by redefining the logical viewport, no rounding in layout, one viewport owner.
- Chapter 15's core prohibitions: no synchronous GL queries in the frame (with G27's exception), fixed-capacity buffers with sub-data updates instead of orphaning (Ken Russell's 2014 guidance still holds: `bufferData(size)` zero-fills), `texStorage2D` plus `texSubImage2D`, separate index buffers, primitive restart index reserved, `#version 300 es` first, `highp` in the fragment shader, no dynamic sampler indexing, uniform blocks fully backed, `alpha: true` with premultiplied output and an opaque clear, `touch-action: none` with Pointer Events, context-loss handling, the WebGPU-shaped boundary of R15.38.
- Chapter 13's principles (never block the observed frame, distributions not averages, `null` for unmeasurable, disjoint frame sections, reasons not totals) are the right ones; G27 to G29 are about the numbers, not the principles.
