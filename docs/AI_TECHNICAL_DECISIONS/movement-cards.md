# Movement cards: the swerve model (DDB-545)

## Date
2026-10-24

## Context

The road model gave rows a job ("pull ahead of a rammer, drop behind to line up a shot"), but flanking was the only way to change slots, so nothing on the board ever used them. Combat Rules now has Swerving (under Game State) and twenty movement cards (Movement cards, under Cards), and raiders get the six that move only themselves (DDB-546, DDB-552).

Before this, `change_position` knew one thing, `position: "flanking"`, and Battle's flank was the only code that moved a vehicle mid-turn. The twenty cards need a one-step swerve in a fixed or picked direction, a move further than one step (to a picked slot, or behind the partner), a swap, a drop-back to the reserved slot, a formation-wide lane switch, and the same moves made on a raider (shoves, Brake Check). Four tasks start from this one in parallel: the fixed-destination batch (DDB-549), the raider-moving batch (DDB-550), slot targeting (DDB-548), and raider swerve planning (DDB-546). So the shape had to cover every card now, even the ones no task here builds, and the core paths had to exist before any card does.

## Options considered

1. **A new effect type per move** (`swerve`, `move_to_slot`, `swap`, `shove`, `drop_back`).
   - Pros: each type reads plainly in card data.
   - Cons: every place that switches on effect types (Battle, BoardProjection, the five AIs, intents, the card validator) grows five cases, and each batch would add its own, all in the same switch statements at the same time.
2. **Card-specific handlers keyed on the card's `type`.**
   - Pros: no data design at all.
   - Cons: the cards stop being data. Raiders get the same cards, upgrades replace the effects list, and the simulator reads effects, so all of it would need special cases.
3. **Extend `change_position` with a discriminator and a few fields (chosen).** `position` says which move, `to` says where, `target` says who moves, `optional` says whether a move that can't happen blocks the card. One parser (`moveSpecOf`) reads it and throws on anything it doesn't know, and the card data check runs it over cards.json.
   - Pros: one effect type, one place that resolves it, every card expressible as data now. Existing flank data keeps working untouched.
   - Cons: a few fields only mean something for some positions (`to` for swerve and move). The parser catches the wrong combinations.

Where the rules live was the other choice. Pure slot geometry (what's adjacent, what's in a formation, what's behind a slot) went in `Road.ts` beside `flankLane` and the lane rules, since it needs no board. Occupancy, reservations, flanking, partners, and destinations need a board, so they went in `BoardProjection`, which already holds the flank rules for both the live board and planning. Battle stays the only thing that moves a live vehicle: it asks a fresh projection to resolve the move, then puts the placements on the vehicles. The projection applies the same placements to its own copy, so play, planning, and the preview share one set of move rules.

## Decision: the change_position shape

| Field | Values | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `position` | `flanking`, `swerve`, `move`, `swap`, `drop_back`, `formation_shift` | Which move. |
| `to` | swerve: `other_lane`, `outside`, `ahead`, `behind`, `pick`, `pick_row`; move: `pick`, `behind_partner` | Where. `pick` is a slot the player picks (any adjacent one for a swerve, any empty formation slot for a move). `pick_row` is a picked row ahead or behind (Shoulder Check). `outside` is the mover's own outside lane, same row. |
| `target` | `self` (default), `target` | Who moves: the caster's vehicle, or the card's target. A swap is always `target` (the vehicle swapped with); a flank and a formation shift are always `self`. |
| `optional` | `true` or absent | An optional move that can't happen is skipped and the rest of the card plays. Any other move with nowhere legal to go makes the card unplayable (call 285), and failing at resolve cancels the rest of the card, as a failed flank does. |
| `on_hit` | `true` or absent | As for any effect: only after an earlier effect of the card landed. A move never rolls to hit itself. |

A card picks at most one slot. Every move obeys the slot rules: a mover that's flanking can't swerve (so shoves never move a flanker or an ambusher), the destination is in the mover's own formation (never onto, off, or along a shoulder), and nobody is in it or holds it reserved, wrecks included. A swerve's destination is also adjacent, range 1, so never diagonal. A drop-back is the one move off a shoulder: a flanker back to its reserved slot.

## The API the other tasks build on

`Road.ts`, pure geometry: `SwerveDirection`, `swerveSlot(from, direction)`, `adjacentFormationSlots(from)`, `formationSlots(team)`, `isFormationMove(team, from, to)`, `isSwerveStep(team, from, to)`, `slotBehind(slot)`, `otherFormationLane(lane)`, `rowAhead(row)`, `rowBehind(row)`.

`Movement.ts`, the shape: `MoveSpec`, `PickedMove`, `moveSpecOf(effect)` (throws on bad data), `movesOf(card)`, `picksSlot(move)`, `pickedMoveOf(card)`, `cardPicksSlot(card)`, `casterMoveOf(card)`.

`BoardProjection`, the board rules: `slotMoveBlocker({ mover, destination, step })`, `moveDestinations({ move, mover })`, `destinationBlocker({ move, mover, destination })`, `swapBlocker({ caster, target })`, `moveBlocker({ card, caster, target?, destination? })`, `landingSlots({ card, caster, destination? })`, `resolveMove({ move, caster, target, destination? })` returning placements or a blocker, `partnerVehicleOf(vehicle)`, `swervedThisTurn(vehicle)`, `wouldDropBack(vehicle)`. `apply` and `targetBlocker` take an optional `destination`; `apply` also takes an optional `actor` (the escort carrying an attack order) and skips an `on_hit` effect when nothing before it landed, by the same range and hit checks play makes.

`Battle`: `playCard({ ..., destination? })`, `getTargetBlocker({ ..., destination? })`, `moveDestinations({ driver, card, target? })`, `needsDestination({ driver, card, target? })`, `getDestinationBlocker({ driver, card, target?, destination })`, `swervedThisTurn(vehicle)`, `getAllIntents({ preview? })` and `projectEnemyTurnStart({ preview? })` with `PlayPreview { driver, card, target?, destination? }`. `getCardBlocker` greys out a swerve card for a flanker or when it has nowhere to go, which the hand already reads through `canPlayCard`. `PlannedAction.destination` and `AIDecision.destination` carry a picked slot through planning and play. `needsDestination` is when slot targeting should ask for a slot: a card with a picked move needs one unless the move is optional and has nowhere legal to go (Shoulder Check on a flanker, or on a raider boxed in ahead and behind), which plays without a slot and skips the shove (call 293). `playCard` requires a destination exactly when it says so.

## The raider-moving batch (DDB-550)

Lay on the Horn, Cut Off, Box In, Brake Check, and Overtake, built on the shape above; the table's "DDB-550's" notes are what this section answers.

- Target conditions are effect `condition`s, as Oil Slick's `target_flanking` already was, read by `BoardProjection.targetConditionBlocker`, which targeting and a raider's planned card both ask: `target_in_formation` (Lay on the Horn, on its shove) and `target_flanking_in_row` (Brake Check, on its drop-back: flanking, in the caster's row). Lay on the Horn's Range 1 sits on its shove, its only base effect, so the card has a range chip and targeting checks it; "in your row" needs no condition of its own (call 320).
- Landing conditions are judged as the effect lands, by `BoardProjection.landingConditionHolds`, which `Battle.applyDamage`, `Battle.applyStatus`, the projection's own landing, and `AimPreview` all ask: `target_ambusher` (Brake Check's 4, after the optional drop-back has had its go, so a flanker with a reserved slot drops back and takes nothing) and `driven_vehicles_within` with a number `within` (Box In's Vulnerable: two driven vehicles on the caster's side, both in reach; calls 321 and 324). A separate field, not `range`, since an effect's `range` is the card's targeting range. Every condition name card data may use is in `EFFECT_CONDITIONS` (BoardProjection.ts); both checks throw on any other, and the card data check holds cards.json to the list, wrappers included.
- Box In's status is `boxed_in` (`BOXED_IN` and `Vehicle.isBoxedIn` in Vehicle.ts), duration 1, so it covers the enemy turn after the card. `BoardProjection.flankBlocker` refuses a boxed-in flanker, and the projection tracks it (`isBoxedIn`, set when the status lands on it), so play, raider planning (`canFlankAnyone`, `orderCarrier`), a planned flank in the preview, and `getCardBlocker`'s greying all honour it from one check. It never moves a flanker (call 322). The plate shows it as a "Boxed in" chip.
- `effectRecipientOf` reads a move's own target: a `change_position` with `target: "target"` lands on the card's target, so base Lay on the Horn (a shove and nothing else) lands on its target for `landsOnTarget` and `AimPreview`, and the aim shows its sure hit. It's still resolved once as the caster's action, and `rollsToHit` never rolls a caster action.
- An upgrade that adds an effect (Lay on the Horn's slow, Brake Check's Vulnerable) can't print it through a `{variable}`, so its `upgrades` block carries its own `summary` and `description`, which `Card` puts on the upgraded copy. The card data check lets only such an upgrade add effects, after the base ones, and holds its texts to the same budgets (Card System Design 1.1).
- Overtake is Run Ahead on the driver's own vehicle: a `speed_boost` of 2 for 2 turns, then `flanking`; DDB-545's `selfSpeedBonus` in the flank checks does the rest. Cut Off is a fixed `swerve` ahead followed by two Range 1 statuses, and its range counts from the landing slot through `landingSlots`.
- The projection needed nothing new for the moves themselves: the shove, the drop-back, the slows, and the boost already land on it, so each card's fizzle shows in the preview as it's aimed and as it's played.
## Evade and board conditions (DDB-549)

The fixed-destination batch (Lane Change, Floor It, Hard Brake, Slipstream, Peel Off, Formation Shift, Weave, Point Man, Tail Gunner) needed nothing new for its moves: every one is a `change_position` the model above already resolves. It added three things.

Evade is a status, `evade_boost`, applied as any status is: `{ "type": "apply_status", "status": "evade_boost", "value": 1, "duration": 1, "target": "self" }` is +1 Evade until the end of the next enemy turn, the duration counting as every status's does. `Vehicle.evadeBoost` sums every `evade_boost` on a vehicle, so they stack (call 312), and `Battle.hitCheck` adds it to the defender's evade for both kinds of check, gunnery against evade plus the card's modifier and ramming against evade. Everything that rolls goes through `hitCheck`, so play, raider planning, the AIs, and the aim preview agree; `HitCheck.evade` and the log's "vs evade" include it. A projection tracks Evade landed on its own board (`BoardProjection.evadeBoostOf`) and passes it to `checkHit` as `evadeBoost`, so a Weave that's only aimed counts in the preview's hit checks. The status row shows it as a buff chip titled with its amount (call 313). Bootlegger Turn's +1 is the same effect with no bonus.

A board condition is a string naming a question about the board, read by `boardConditionOf` in `EffectConditions.ts`, which throws on one it doesn't know: `caster_row_<ahead|center|behind>` (the caster's vehicle is in that row), `target_row_<ahead|behind|same>` (the target's row against the caster's), and `caster_swerved` (the caster's vehicle has changed slot this turn, `swervedThisTurn`). Rows are read on every lane, shoulders included (call 310). `conditionHolds(condition, { casterSlot, targetSlot, casterSwerved })` answers it from slots alone, so the battle, a projection, and the aim preview each pass their own board. The batch uses them as a conditional bonus on an effect's value: `bonus` added to `value` while `bonus_condition` holds, read by `effectValue(effect, board)` wherever a damage or status value lands (Battle's damage and statuses, the projection's statuses, the aim preview's damage). Point Man is damage 1 with `bonus` 3 on `caster_row_ahead`, Tail Gunner damage 1 with `bonus` 2 on `target_row_ahead`, Weave Evade 1 with `bonus` 1 on `caster_swerved`. The card data check runs `effectBonusOf` over every effect, so a half-written pair or an unknown condition fails there. DDB-550's conditions (`effect.condition`, read by `BoardProjection.targetConditionBlocker` and `landingConditionHolds`) decide whether a target is legal or an effect lands at all; a bonus condition only changes an amount, so it's its own field, outside `EFFECT_CONDITIONS` and checked by `effectBonusOf` instead. The names don't overlap, and since these read slots alone, a landing or target condition can hand a row or swerve question to `conditionHolds` rather than repeat it. A raider's intent amount (`Battle.previewDamage`) doesn't add bonuses yet, since no raider card has one.

An upgrade the base text can't say rewrites both texts in its upgrade block, `upgrades.summary` and `upgrades.description`, the shape DDB-550 uses for Lay on the Horn and Brake Check. Slipstream's draw is an effect added after the base ones, as theirs are. Floor It's longer boost is a `{turns}` variable on the status's `duration`, printed only by the upgraded text, so the card data check's upgrade test still covers it. Formation Shift drops Exhaust with `upgrades.tags`, which replace the base tags. The data check requires a rewriting upgrade to change both texts, rather than to add an effect, since Floor It and Formation Shift add none.

## The slot-pick batch (DDB-551)

Juke, Bootlegger Turn, Strafe, Fall In, Leapfrog, and Shoulder Check went in as data in the table's shapes, and slot targeting (DDB-548) took them with no screen change; its screen tests, aim preview tests, and gallery scenes now use these cards from cards.json, and the stand-ins it was built against are gone. Two things in the data differ from those stand-ins. Strafe's damage rolls gunnery against evade, as any ranged attack without "Always hits" does (call 342), where the stand-in always hit. Fall In's upgrade adds a `gain_shield` of 3 on the target after the move, so its upgrade block rewrites both texts, as Lay on the Horn's does. Bootlegger Turn's Evade is DDB-549's `evade_boost` with value 1 and no bonus, and Shoulder Check is a `ramming` damage with Range 1 and no `condition`, so a flanker or a boxed-in raider takes the hit and stays put (call 293).

The one rule change is in the hand's check. `BoardProjection.moveBlocker` with no target used to judge only the caster's own moves, and a target's once it was known. Leapfrog and Fall In move vehicles on your own side only, so the hand can know whether either has anyone to move, and call 285 says a move with nowhere legal to go greys out before you commit (call 341). Without a target, a swap now blocks when no vehicle passes `swapBlocker` ("Rig has nobody to swap with"), and a move of the card's target that isn't optional blocks when nothing the card could name has a destination (`moveCandidates`, by target type: "Fall In has no escort that can move"). Optional target moves (the shoves, Brake Check) never block, as before. `Battle.getCardBlocker` used to skip every order's moves, since an attack order's flank is its carrier's; it now runs `moveBlocker` for orders too, which judges only Fall In's escort move and leaves the caster-side flank checks to cards that aren't orders.

Each card's fizzles come from the paths already there: a swerve, move, swap, or shove that takes a vehicle out of a planned raider card's range drops the intent from the preview as the card is aimed at its slot (`getAllIntents({ preview })` with the destination) and fizzles it in the enemy turn. Fall In does it for the escort a raider aimed at, Leapfrog for whichever of the two vehicles was the target, and Shoulder Check for the raider's own card. Bootlegger Turn's Evade lands in the projection too, so a planned shot it turns into a miss reads that way in the hit check.

## Raider swerves (DDB-546)

Raiders swerve from movement cards in their own decks under the same rules as the player, in their own formation; a flanker can't, since `getCardBlocker` already greys a swerve out for it on the planning board. The real raider decks are DDB-552's, so the tests give a raider the cards by hand: Lane Change, Floor It, Hard Brake, and Cut Off from cards.json, Juke and Strafe as test-only cards in the table's shape until their batch lands.

- The plan names every swerve's slot. `PlannedAction.destination` is now where the card's own swerve or move takes the raider: the slot the AI picked for a picked move, or for a fixed one the slot its direction gives on the planning board (`AIController.plannedDestination`). An AI that plans a picked move without a slot, or with one the move can't take (`destinationBlocker`), throws, rather than leaving the plan to read the first slot it could pick or naming a slot that fizzles for no reason.
- Planning already tracked a swerve: `AIController` applies each pick to the shared projection, and `BoardProjection.apply` moves the raider, so the cards after it are judged from the destination, as after a flank. What was missing was choosing one. `AIPlayer.generatePossibleActions` now offers a picked move once per slot it could take (`destinationsFor`), and a targeted one (Strafe) only at the slots its own range reaches the target from, so every AI picks slots, player stand-ins included.
- Scoring a swerve like a flank, what it puts in range and what it takes out, is `ai/SwerveEstimate.ts`: each other card in hand that's affordable after the swerve and reaches out from the raider's slot (a Range attack or debuff aimed at the other side) counts its damage, or 1 for a debuff, when someone it could hit is in range from one slot and not the other. `swerveScore` turns that into one number: what it opens, at the AI's weight per point of damage, when it strands nothing, and otherwise minus what it strands, so the swerve waits until the cards it would strand are played and then counts for what it opens. The aggressive AI adds it at its damage weight; MCTS adds it in `evaluateMovement` at its own. Both AIs otherwise play anything that does something before ending the turn, so a swerve that opens nothing on a card with nothing else worth playing (`aimlessSwerve`: no damage, status, Shield, armor, repair, adrenaline, or a draw that keeps a card) scores under ending the turn; without that, a raider with two Lane Changes and nothing to shoot swerved out and back. MCTS also holds the turn open for a move only when it flanks, opens something from some slot it could take (`bestSwerveGain`), or sits on a card with more to it. A boxed-in raider can't flank, so its flank card greys out on the planning board and it swerves into range instead when it can. It reads only this turn's cards because close-up re-forms the raiders across from you at the end of the enemy turn, so where a swerve leaves a raider rarely lasts into your turn. Both AIs (and the ramming AI) now give the flank priority only to a card that flanks; before, any `change_position` got it.
- Stale destinations (call 289) are `BoardProjection.plannedMoveBlocker`, which `getPlannedCardBlocker` runs in place of `moveBlocker`. A picked move's slot has to be one it could still pick (next to the raider and empty, for a swerve); a fixed one's has to be where its direction still goes (call 330). A card with no target, which play sent straight to apply and the preview never checked, now runs the same check in both: play logs the fizzle and spends the card, the preview drops the intent, and the cards after it are judged from where the raider really is.
- The move intent (call 288) is `IntentType.MOVE`, ranked after defend (call 331), with `Intent.destination` on every intent whose card moves the raider, shown whatever the tier (call 332). A move intent's label is whatever else its card does (`buffLabelOf`, which now labels only a flanking move "flank" and skips other moves, so Floor It reads Fast and Juke Draw).
- On screen, the pill is `move`: the swerve icon (`alt_route`, a new glyph in the icon atlas) and the label, in a muted raider red, the attack's hue at lower strength, so it reads as the raiders' without reading as a hit. Any other pill whose intent carries a destination leads with the swerve icon before its own, 20 wider, so a Strafe and a Strafe-less shot look different in the row. `RoadView` outlines every planned destination (`PlannedMoveOutlines`): a solid 2 px raider-red border with a faint fill and the swerve icon at the middle, inset like the empty slots' dashes, drawn under the tokens and under slot targeting's slot targets (DDB-548), which are the road's children. Both are red on the raiders' side, and a hovered target is solid, so while slot targets show the outlines recede to a faint border and icon and never read as a chosen slot. It comes from the intents, so it drops the moment the preview drops the swerve, and `showVehicles` drops the stored plan of any vehicle it's no longer passed, so a raider that leaves the fight takes its outline with it. Two raiders planning the same slot is one outline. A raider can plan into the slot another raider's earlier swerve leaves; that outline sits under the first raider's token until it moves.
- The tooltip says where: "Swerves to enemy inside, center" for a move, and the usual line with ", after it swerves to ..." for a card that swerves first.
- Gallery: `combat-move-intent`, the road with a Lane Change move pill, a Strafe's attack pill led by the swerve icon, both destinations outlined, and a raider that stays put.

## Consequences

- One move path. `Battle.applyMove` resolves through `BoardProjection.resolveMove` and logs each vehicle's move; `relocate` is the one place a card's move sets a slot, and it records `swervedThisTurn`. The flank goes through it too. The set clears as each turn starts.
- Hand greying needed no screen change. The combat screen already asks `Battle.canPlayCard`, which reads `getCardBlocker`, the same path DDB-533 used for Flank.
- Range for a card that moves first (Strafe, Cut Off) counts from where it lands, in targeting (`targetBlocker` through `landingSlots`) and in a raider's planned card (`getPlannedCardBlocker`). Until a slot is picked, a target is legal if any slot the card could pick reaches it, which is what slot targeting wants for highlighting raiders.
- The preview already follows a move once it lands, because `projectEnemyTurnStart` builds from the live board. `getAllIntents({ preview })` also judges a card that's only aimed, applying its moves and speed changes to the projection first, so DDB-548's hover can drop intents that would fizzle before the drop. An attack order lands through the escort that would carry it out (`orderCarrier`), and an `on_hit` move lands only if the hit check before it passes, so the preview never shows a shove a miss would skip. Drop-backs in that projection read projected speeds (`wouldDropBack`), so a boost on the aimed card counts.
- Heavy needed nothing new: a movement card's speed is a `speed_boost` status, and `Vehicle.speedChange` already drops boosts on a Heavy vehicle (heavy-armor.md). The move still happens.
- Moving never spends an escort: `relocate` doesn't touch `spent`, and Fall In carries no `spendsEscort`.
- The road view animates any change of slot as a swerve, so swerves, named-slot moves, and swaps needed no change; RoadView tests cover all three, a swap moving both tokens at once.
- The `[Swerve]` keyword's auto-highlight would have caught Flank and Flanking Maneuver's full text ("swerve onto their shoulder"), which isn't a swerve under these rules, so both now say "pull onto". The Flank keyword's own text changed to match.
- A planned raider card with a target whose own move has gone stale (its picked slot taken or no longer next to it, its fixed slot blocked, or the raider flanking by then) fizzles: `getPlannedCardBlocker` runs `moveBlocker` first, so the preview drops it and play logs the fizzle (call 289). A planned card with no target (a raider's Lane Change, Floor It, Hard Brake, Juke) runs `plannedMoveBlocker` on its own in both (DDB-546, above).
- Slot targeting (DDB-548, [slot-targeting.md](./slot-targeting.md)) reads every step from the card's effects (`aimStepsOf`): a picked move of the caster asks for its slot before any target (Strafe), a picked move of the target after it (Fall In, Shoulder Check), and a swap goes through vehicle targeting (Leapfrog). The slot-pick batch needs no screen change if its cards keep this table's shapes.
