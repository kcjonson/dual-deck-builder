# The battle state, its actions, and its events

## Date
2026-10-30

## Context

The first combat slice of the C++ port, in `libs/rules/battle/`: a fight set up from content and played with the simple cards. [rules-ai-and-packs.md](./rules-ai-and-packs.md) fixes the shape, a `BattleState` value with `legalActions` and `apply`, and this record covers what that shape needed decided to be built: who acts during the enemy turn before intents exist, what `apply` hands back, and what happens to the cards whose effects aren't in yet.

## Options considered

For the enemy turn:

1. **The raiders as one side, any of them acting at any time.** Simple, but it's a whole-team choice, which the packs record rules out.
2. **Each raider in turn, its own behavior choosing its plays (chosen first),** then the same seam moved to planning: each raider's policy chooses on a rehearsal of the enemy turn while the player's turn starts, and the enemy turn plays the plans.

For planning against the board ahead, the TypeScript kept a second model, `BoardProjection`, that tracked slots, flanks, and speed and re-read every effect it needed. Planning on a copy of the fight with `apply` needs nothing of its own, since the copy plays exactly as the fight will (chosen).

For cards the rules can't play yet (statuses, movement, orders):

1. **Resolve what they can and skip the rest.** That's the TypeScript's conditional effects, which never landed (DDB-121), and the kind of quiet gap the alpha doesn't keep.
2. **Never a legal play, with the reason (chosen).** A card is playable only when every effect, condition, and target it has is implemented, so nothing a played card does is skipped.

## Decision

- **State.** Drivers and vehicles are two vectors that keep every entry for the whole fight, players first, and refer to each other by index. A driver's numbers (skills, HP, adrenaline, hand limit) are copied in at setup, since a campaign driver comes in hurt and a mod can change a limit; cards stay `CardId`s into `Content`. The fight's randomness is `RandomStreams`, one stream per seat's deck (`deck:player:0`), and shuffles are the only draws today: the hit check is a comparison, as the targeting preview already says.
- **Turns.** Each player turn opens in `Planning`: the statuses tick, every hand still held is discarded, everyone's adrenaline refills, Shield clears, and five are dealt, then the raiders commit their plans and the player plays from either hand until `EndTurn`. That one `EndTurn` plays the whole enemy turn from the plans, raider by raider in seat order, and opens the next player turn, so a fight never rests mid-enemy-turn and a renderer paces it from the events. A raider acts only from the wheel; one riding as a passenger draws but doesn't plan, as in the TypeScript. A stunned raider skips its turn. Each side's turn ends with flankers that are no longer faster than the vehicle they outran dropping back, and the wrecks clearing; the raiders' then closes up.
- **Planning** (enemy-intent-planning.md). The rules define a `Policy`, `choose(board, content, actions, rng)`, and `planEnemyTurn` takes the policy for each raider seat, read off the board it plans on, since a raider arriving in a later wave takes a seat the fight didn't start with; the rules never include or link `libs/ai`, which implements them. A raider plans on a rehearsal: a copy of the fight with the player's turn ended there, so drop-backs and death marks have happened, in the `EnemyTurn` phase, where `actingRaider` names the raider and `legalActions` offers its plays. Each pick is applied to the rehearsal before the next, so a raider plans after its own flank and after the raiders before it, and the plan records the card, its target, occupant, and picked slot, and where its own move takes the raider. A policy's random picks draw from `plan:raiders:<seat>`. The enemy turn plays each step without choosing again: a card no longer in hand, a target wrecked with nobody to follow, a play `actionBlocker` refuses, or a move that no longer lands where it was planned fizzles and is spent; a card aimed at a wreck follows the first of its survivors still in the fight; a raider wrecked or left without its driver drops the rest.
- **Intents** are `intentsOf`: the enemy turn as it would play if the player ended the turn now, run with `apply` on a copy and read off its events, step by step. A step that would fizzle shows nothing, a hit shows what it would deal now (a Vulnerable picked up since planning raises it), and a hit check that would miss shows as a miss. Each has a kind (attack, debuff, defend, move, buff, ranked as call 331 has them), its target or that it hits every enemy, and where its move goes. An elite or a boss hides its card, its numbers, and its statuses, and shows the rest.
- **Statuses** live on the vehicles in the state, each application its own with a value and the player turn starts it has left, or none for the rest of the fight. They count down at the start of the player's turn, as enemy-intent-planning.md has them, so one put on during the player's turn lasts through the enemy turn after it. Speed boosts and slows add to speed, but a Heavy vehicle (armor past its base) drops the boosts; Evade adds to what a hit check has to beat; Vulnerable takes half again; a stunned raider skips its part of the enemy turn, and one whose stun just wore off is Steady and shakes off the next until the player turn after; triple_damage and damage_bonus raise the vehicle's attacks; a death_mark takes whoever is at the wheel when its side's turn ends; boxed_in stops a new flank. The spec only says what a stun does to a raider, so a card that would stun one of the player's vehicles isn't a legal play.
- **Movement** (movement-cards.md and Combat Rules' Flanking and Swerving are the rules). A vehicle on a shoulder carries its flank state, the slot it left (kept reserved) and the vehicle it outran, or neither for an ambusher, which a raider seat can start as in a row with one of the player's vehicles. Every change_position move plays, from `Movement.h`, which targeting and play both ask, so a move is legal by the rule it's carried out by. A card's own moves walk the caster along as targeting reads the card, so range counts from where they leave it (Strafe, Cut Off) and a flank counts the boost before it (Overtake). Target conditions (target_flanking, target_flanking_in_row, target_in_formation) decide who the card can be played at and are checked again as each effect lands; target_ambusher and driven_vehicles_within only decide whether that effect lands. A vehicle a card moves has swerved this turn, which caster_swerved reads; the drop-back and close-up between turns don't count. A move that has to happen and can't, at play, cancels the rest of the card; an optional one is skipped.
- **Actions.** `PlayCard` names the driver, the hand index, the target vehicle for a card played at one, for a card that lands on one person aboard (Medical Kit) which of the driver and passenger, and for a card whose move picks its slot (Juke, Strafe, Shoulder Check) which slot. `legalActions` lists every play `actionBlocker` passes, each target, occupant, and slot spelled out, then `EndTurn`. `apply` throws on anything `actionBlocker` refuses, and `cardBlocker` says why a card can't be played at all, for the hand to grey it out.
- **Events.** `apply` returns what happened in order, each event saying what changed and by how much: draws, burns, shuffles, the hit check's numbers, where a hit went (Shield, armor, structure, then each occupant), who went down, took the wheel, jumped aboard, or crashed out, a vehicle carrying on as an escort, every change of slot and why, a move that couldn't happen, statuses going on and wearing off, stuns skipped and shaken off, death marks taken, wrecks leaving the road, and the fight's end. The log words them; a renderer animates them.
- **Escorts and orders** (Combat Rules, Escorts; escorts.md). The setup seats the convoy's escorts after the driven vehicles, up to four in roster order, each with the structure it carries and full armor, in its type's preferred slot or the next free one. An escort has its crew's skills, no driver, one passenger seat, and a spent flag every player turn clears; a vehicle carrying on as an escort starts spent (decision 43). An attack order is carried by the nearest ready escort, of its type for a signature order, from which the whole play is legal (range, flank, and conditions are the escort's), with the spec's ties; it acts as that escort and spends it if the card says so. A signature card needs a living escort of its type, and an attack order or Rally the Convoy a ready one. Buff and move orders target an escort, ready or spent, and Draw Fire spends it. Rally the Convoy fires each ready escort at its own nearest raider in reach, picked again for each, then spends them all. Draw Fire keeps covers in play order until the end of the enemy turn: a planned card aimed at another of the player's vehicles in a covered row goes to the row's last living cover if it can reach it, and keeps its target otherwise; the intents read it. A looter prefers the convoy's haulers. Dividends are the campaign's, read off the haulers still running after a won fight, so the battle pays none.
- **What plays.** Every effect the card data has, every condition an effect or a bonus carries, and every target type but both_drivers and any, which no card uses. A card with a move inside a conditional or a second picked slot isn't a legal play either. That leaves all 47 of the base pack's cards playable.
- **Small readings of the card format**, where the TypeScript was quiet or did the same: `scaling` adds nothing to damage, and ramming scaling only picks the ramming check, as the card texts read; a hit modifier raises the evade a ram checks against too, though no ram has one; a card's target has to be within the range of every effect landing on it, where the TypeScript took the longest, which no card tells apart; and an effect on someone an earlier effect of the same card took out of the fight does nothing.
- **The first policies** (`libs/ai`, `ddb::ai`, which links `ddb::rules` and nothing else; `ddb_check_layering()` holds both directions). First playable is the first play on offer. The aggressive flanker, every base raider's, scores each offer by applying it to a copy of the board: the armor, structure, and HP the other side loses (more the more hurt the target already is), a kill, pulling onto the shoulder or a boost that opens a flank, a status on the other side, armor and repairs, adrenaline, and for the player's side its Shield and the cards a draw keeps (worth nothing to a raider, call 355). It never gives up a flank it would hold, and a play that does nothing scores under ending the turn. Its weights are named (`damage`, `kill`, `flank`, `heal`, `defense`, `debuff`, `draw`) and an opponent's behavior can set any of them. A raider's target preference is applied before any policy chooses: a killer's card that lands on a driven vehicle is only played at one. `OpponentPolicies` binds each opponent's behavior once, and an unknown policy, an unknown weight, or a script fails as a ContentError; `forRaiders` looks each raider's up by its opponent on the board it's asked about.
- **How a fight ends** is in Waves and how fights end, below.

## Provisional calls

Pending Kevin's approval in [gameplay-calls.md](../gameplay-calls.md) (The battle state).

- 372: Coordinated Attack's partner_attacked holds when the partner has played a card tagged attack this turn, whether it hit or missed, because the card says "attacked", and a miss is still the partner taking their shot.
- 373: a raider has no partner, so partner_attacked never holds for one, because raiders each act alone and no raider deck carries the card.
- 374: Precision Shot's Speed+ is a speed_boost, so its data reads `self_has_status speed_boost` and speed_plus is gone from the rules, because Card System Design 2.2 has Nitro Boost giving Speed+ and nothing ever gave speed_plus; the bonus lands while the boost is on the vehicle, even when Heavy cancels its speed.
- 375: each application of a status is its own and they stack, boosts, slows, Evade, and damage bonuses adding up and the rest on or off, each wearing off on its own turns, because that's how the cards read, and nothing says a second Nitro Boost refreshes the first.
- 376: an attack's damage takes the attacker's damage_bonus, then its triple_damage, then flanking's half again, then the target's Vulnerable, rounding down at each, and none of them touch a cost a card charges its own caster, because the attacker's own modifiers come before the target's, and Combat Rules already keeps Berserker's cost flat.
- 377: Witness Me's death_mark takes whoever is at the wheel of the marked vehicle at the end of that side's turn to 0 HP, so a player's driver is down and can still be revived if the partner wins, while a passenger who then takes the wheel rides on, because "die at end of turn" is the driver's, and Kevin's rule makes a player's death in a fight a down.
- 378: triple_damage triples every attack from the vehicle for as long as it lasts, the rest of the turn for a player's Witness Me, while damage_bonus (Flanking Maneuver's) is used up by the vehicle's next attack card, hit or miss, because Witness Me says "all damage this turn" and Flanking Maneuver says "next attack".
- 379: a flanker whose reserved slot trades lanes in a Formation Shift hasn't swerved, so its Weave gets no +1 for it, because its own slot on the shoulder never changed, and call 290 counts a change of slot.
- 380: a partner riding as a passenger in your own vehicle has no vehicle of their own, so Slipstream has nobody to slip behind and Leapfrog nobody to swap with, because both cards move you against another vehicle, and call 292 makes the partner's vehicle the one they're in.
- 381: a planned card that fizzles goes to discard and costs its adrenaline, as much as the raider has left, because "it's spent", and a raider shouldn't come out of a fizzle with adrenaline its plan never had.
- 382: a planned flank fizzles when it would pull onto a different row of the shoulder than planned, because the plan named where it goes, and call 330 already fizzles a fixed swerve whose slot has moved.
- 383: a raider can plan the cards its own draws bring during its plan, because the rehearsal draws exactly what the enemy turn will from the raider's own stream; no raider deck draws yet.
- 384: a basic raider's intent shows a hit that its check will miss as a miss, with no damage, and an elite's hides it with the rest, because the checks have no dice and the projection knows, as the targeting preview already shows MISS for your own cards.
- 385: vehicles carrying on as escorts fire after the convoy's own in Rally the Convoy, in seat order rather than the order they converted, because decision 42 puts them last and two drivers going down in one fight is rare enough that their order between themselves hasn't earned bookkeeping.

## Consequences

- Every base card plays, every starter and raider deck plays whole, the base raiders plan their turns with the aggressive flanker, and a fight can be fielded from any base encounter and played to its end. What's left is the other policies, the player stand-ins first, then the balance sim.
- The aggressive flanker scores only the play in front of it, so it never swerves to line up a shot for a later card, as the TypeScript's swerve-reach estimate did; a raider deck's bare swerves (none today, call 354) would go unplayed.
- Coordinated Attack's second hit lands for a pair, closing what [solo-driver-fights.md](./solo-driver-fights.md) calls DDB-121's gap, and reads false for a lone driver, as that record's call 1 wants.
- A preview is `apply` on a copy, and the tests hold a copied fight to replaying exactly as the original.

## Waves and how fights end

The sixth slice: encounters as pack content, a fight fielded from one, its waves arriving, and how a fight ends. Combat Rules (Reinforcements, How a fight ends), Runs and Encounters (How big, Encounters), and [raider-waves-and-fight-ends.md](./raider-waves-and-fight-ends.md) are the rules.

### Decision

- **Encounters are pack content**, `encounters/*.json` holding `{"encounters": [...]}` as the card folders do, read strictly into `EncounterDefinition` (`core/Encounter.h`) and overridden by id under the same rules. The TypeScript's file kept one road cap per skull count beside its templates, and a table like that has no id for a pack to override it by, so each template carries its own `roadCap`, the base ones their skull count's 3, 4, and 5. A template has at least two waves, since a fight is reinforced partway through and game stage grows the last; the first has no timing and fits under the cap; every raider and the grow raider name an opponent.
- **Fielding a fight.** `pickEncounter` picks among `encountersFor` a skull count and tier (tier 5 borrowing tier 4's, call 202) on the fight's seed, from an `encounter` stream the fight never draws on, so a seed fields the same encounter and the pick moves no shuffle. `fieldEncounter` fills a setup's raider side: the first wave, the rest queued, the road cap, and the stage, which queues one more grow raider in the last wave every two stages; a lone driver's half comes off the last wave first (call 204). `startBattle` grows every raider 5% a stage, rounding half up, its structure always and its HP unless it's a mook, and builds the queued raiders then too, so a bad id fails the setup rather than an arrival.
- **The queue is part of the state.** A queued raider is a whole `BattleDriver` and `BattleVehicle`, grown, its deck in list order, and takes a seat at the end of both lists as it pulls in. Nothing already in the fight moves, so every index, deck stream, and plan stream stays put, and the arrival shuffles on its new seat's stream. Seating every wave at the start, off the road, would have left every rule that reads `onRoad` telling a queued raider from a wreck.
- **Due and arrival.** As each player turn opens, the next wave is due if the raiders in the fight are at or under its arrival count, or its turn count has passed since the last wave first played (call 193). That's before the raiders plan rather than after, which changes nothing, since planning moves no one on the real road. A road left with no raider in the fight announces the wave whenever it happens, in either turn. The wave lands at the end of the enemy turn, after close-up: the free formation slots in opening order, each lane from the row the latest raider out left (a flanker's reserved row), as many as the road cap leaves room for, the rest still due. Events are `WaveDue` and `RaiderArrived`, then the arrival's `DeckShuffled`. `arrivalsOf` is the top bar's "+2 arriving" and the road's row marks, read off the enemy turn played on a copy as the intents are, and `waveOnRoad` is its "Wave 2 of 3".
- **Planning with arrivals.** `PolicyFor` takes the board with the seat, since an arrival's seat didn't exist when the fight started, and `OpponentPolicies::forRaiders` reads the raider's opponent off it.
- **How a fight ends** (`endIfOver`, after every card and enemy step). Lost once no player's driver is in the fight, a last hit that takes out both sides included. Won once no raider is on the road or queued, or by a rout once a vehicle that `leads` is out of the fight (call 206) or the nominal threat in the fight and queued is at or under a quarter of `startingThreat`, the opening wave's and the queue's; the rest flee and the queue never arrives. A raider's `threat` and `leads` are copied onto its vehicle at setup, as a driver's numbers are. A road with no raider left and a wave queued isn't over.
- **Break-off.** `Damage` marks the turn when a raider loses armor, structure, or HP during the player's turn, a hit its Shield takes all of aside (calls 184 and 197). The player's `EndTurn` takes their death marks, then counts the turn: a quiet one adds to `quietTurns`, a hurt raider sets it back to 0, and at three the fight ends broken off with no enemy turn. The rehearsal raiders plan on never breaks off, since the player hasn't played the turn yet.
- **The result** is `outcome` (won, lost, or broken off) and, for a won fight the raiders fled, `rout` (the leader out, or the threat thinned), both in `FightEnded` as well; the campaign reads the drivers, vehicles, and escorts off the state as before.
- **Every fight routs and breaks off.** The TypeScript made break-off opt-in for its test harnesses and AI evaluators, which bounded fights by turn count and read anything not won as lost, while every fight the game played had it on. Nothing here bounds a fight by turns, so a fight seated straight from raiders routs and breaks off by the same rules, its starting threat the raiders it opened with.

### Provisional calls

- 386: when ending the turn now would break the fight off, the raiders' intents show nothing and no arrivals are marked, though their plans stand, because both are that enemy turn played out on a copy and a broken-off fight has none; a hit on a raider brings them back.
- 387: "+N arriving" and its row marks count what pulls in if the player ends the turn now, read off that enemy turn on a copy, so a wave that only comes because the raiders empty the road themselves shows too, and one a rout would stop shows nothing, because call 207 has the readout promise what the player will see.

### Consequences

- A fight can be fielded from any base encounter at any tier and stage, for one driver or two, and played to won, lost, or broken off. The base encounters and raiders are the TypeScript's, untuned against these rules; that waits on the balance sim.
- A queued raider costs a full driver and vehicle in every copy of the fight, a few dozen at most, which a lookahead copies with the rest.
- Nothing words the events yet: a "3 raiders arriving" line reads `arrivalsOf`, since `WaveDue` names only the wave.
